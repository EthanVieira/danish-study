# Level B2: Vantage

**Prerequisite:** Milestone B1 reached (step B1-1093 ticked). The README methods still apply, and the B1 patterns continue in the heavier B2 form defined under Patterns below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow extended speech, television news and current-affairs debate, films in standard Danish and most natural conversation between Danes; read contemporary literary prose, newspaper opinion pieces and official documents with ease; take part in discussion with fluency, argue a position, defend it under questioning and use the modal particles and discourse markers that make speech sound Danish; and write clear, well-structured texts of 300 to 500 words in the right register, including a formal complaint, a review, a summary and a *læserbrev*. Vocabulary about 8000 words. Grammar is complete and mostly automatic; remaining work is register, nuance and error repair. This is the level of Prøve i Dansk 3 and of most Danish workplaces.

**Time:** about 960 hours over 36 guide weeks: about 830 hours in the 1327 numbered steps (of which about 35 hours are checks and about 88 hours are Casual listening steps that fit a commute or chores), about 100 hours of Anki (roughly 25 minutes a day at this deck size) and about 30 hours of dagbog. A typical guide week has 21–26 hours of steps, which is six study days of about four hours; Weeks 20 and 30, the three milestone weeks and the exit week run to 27–34. Week 1 and the last two weeks are lighter. About a third of the level is the recurring weekly work (native podcasts, news, voice sessions, writing, songs); about half of all the time is Danish watched, heard or read for meaning. A guide week is a unit of work, not a calendar promise: at 2 hours a day the level takes about 64 calendar weeks; keep the order.

**What changes at B2** (Appendix A): everything is native material. Grammar is studied in Danish (*Grammatikken*) and then in depth in English (*Danish: A Comprehensive Grammar*). *Borgen* and *Forbrydelsen* replace *Matador*. Literature is the centre: Tove Ditlevsen's trilogy completed, Adler-Olsen, Helle Helle, Rifbjerg, Scherfig, a contemporary memoir and Yahya Hassan. Non-fiction is the citizenship-test's sister material on democracy and everyday life, DR's *Historien om Danmark*, Zetland and a daily newspaper. Speaking adds debate and news retelling. Writing reaches 300 to 500 words and the *kronik*.

**How a day works at B2** (README, "A daily session"): first the two dailies. Clear Anki (20 new cards a day, at least half of them sentence cards) and write the dagbog (10 sentences on six days of every week; from Week 10, three of the six entries are a 100-word reflection on something you read or heard). Then do the next unticked step, and the one after it if you have time. Everything else at B2 is a numbered step: every reading sitting, every episode, every podcast, every voice session and every listening sitting has its place in the order, and nothing runs in the background. Reading, series and listening are scheduled like this (Method 6): the set books and a small free-reading shelf (SHELF below) put a "Reading:" step in every week; the casual series is *Badehotellet*, then *Klovn*, then *Krøniken*, then *Badehotellet* again in the exit weeks; the "Listening:" steps re-hear the chapters you read (the re-listen rule in the Book pattern) and add *Fasandræberne* as an audiobook and *P1 Dokumentar*. The one-in-two rule starts here: the full episode pattern applies to every second *Borgen* episode and to one *Forbrydelsen* episode of each pair; the others are watched straight through. Every book with an adaptation is followed by its film.

## Patterns

Every recurring activity is defined once here. A step that names a pattern is done exactly as the pattern says, in one sitting, and it is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done.

**Genstart pattern**: the newest *Genstart* episode you have not heard, once through with no pausing (extensive). Then write a 3-sentence note in Danish of its argument in the notebook.

**Retelling pattern** (in the *Genstart* step that says "with retelling"): after the note, put five keywords on paper and record a 3-minute oral retelling of the episode without stopping. Name the file by the step ID and listen back once.

**Long-programme pattern**: the episode the step names, straight through (extensive); when an episode runs longer than an hour, stop after the first hour. Then write an 8-sentence summary in Danish and note the time stamp of the five minutes you understood least. The **second listen** is a step of its own on a later day: replay those five minutes, pausing to write down every phrase you can catch.

**TV Avisen pattern**: the most recent evening edition of *TV Avisen* on DRTV, no subtitles, no pausing. Write five headlines in Danish, then check them against dr.dk/nyheder.

**Voice-session pattern**: 20 minutes in AI voice mode with the saved B2 instruction (B2-0006). The step names the kind of session:
- *topic*: the monologue topic the step names (Appendix F part 2). Give your view and let the assistant question it. This session prepares the monologue that follows it.
- *debate*: the motion the step names (Appendix F part 3), with the saved debate instruction added. State your real opinion in your first sentence; the assistant argues the other side.
- *book and screen*: tell the assistant what has happened in the book the step names and in the last episode you watched; it asks critical follow-up questions.
- *news*: retell two stories from this week's *Genstart* and *TV Avisen* and give your opinion on each.
- *roleplay*: the roleplay the step names (Appendix F part 4), with the assistant in the other role.

A voice session is finished when you have read the assistant's error list and written its three "a Dane would have said" expressions in the notebook.

**Monologue pattern**: Recording protocol, 5 minutes, on the topic the step names (Appendix F part 2); transcribe it and run the Correction protocol.

**Dictation pattern**: take the audio of the Zetland article the step names and start one minute in. Play 2 minutes of it sentence by sentence, writing every word. Compare with the article's text, count the wrong or missing words as a percentage, rewrite each wrong word three times, and log the percentage in `writing/dictations.md`.

**Bank-text pattern**: the Appendix I task the step names: about 300 words unless the task says otherwise, closed book; Correction protocol; clean copy with the error count in `writing/portfolio-B2.md`. When a task comes round a second time (Weeks 21–31), write the new error count next to the first version's.

**Reflection pattern**: 100 words in Danish on the *Krøniken* episode the step names; Correction protocol.

**Newspaper pattern**: two articles, each read with at most 6 lookups and followed by a two-sentence summary in Danish. In a week's first sitting: the two top stories on the front page of your newspaper. In its second sitting: the top story of the newspaper's culture section and the top story on dr.dk/nyheder.

**Dagbog-correction pattern**: run the week's dagbog entries through the Correction protocol in one go and copy the corrected sentences into the notebook. Then the harvest: take five idioms or collocations from the week's input (your notes on the episodes, articles and books) and make an Anki card for each.

**Song pattern**: the Appendix J song the step names: listen twice, read the lyrics and look up every word, then sing along until you know it by heart.

**Zetland pattern**: the newest Zetland article you have not heard: listen to the journalist's reading with no text, then read the article. One tracker line.

**MED pattern**: the pages the step names in *Medborgerskabsprøven – demokrati og hverdagsliv i Danmark*: the audio of the pages first, then read them; a 2-sentence summary in Danish for each fact sheet you finish; then the number of cards the step names.

**Debatten pattern**: the newest *Debatten* (DR2) on DRTV, extensive, no pausing. Then take the six minutes from 20:00 to 26:00, rewatch them twice, write each side's three main claims in Danish, and record a 2-minute statement of your own position.

**History-article pattern**: search danmarkshistorien.dk (Aarhus University's free Danish history site) for the term the step names and read the main article on it, extensive; 5 cards.

**Listening pattern** (every step titled "Listening:"): audio only, no text, no tasks, nothing to write. These steps are Casual and are made for a commute, a walk or chores, but they are done at their place in the order like any other step. There are four kinds:
- *re-listen*: the audiobook of the pages the step names, which you read in an earlier step. This is the **re-listen rule**: everything you read at the desk, intensive or extensive, is heard again as audio only within 48 hours. When the step gives a time limit, stop there. If a shelf book has no audiobook, play the newest *P1 Dokumentar* episode you have not heard instead.
- *Genstart again*: the most recent *Genstart* episode you did with the Genstart pattern, once more.
- *P1 Dokumentar*: the newest episode in DR Lyd that you have not heard (self-contained radio documentaries of about 40–55 minutes).
- *Fasandræberne*: the part of the audiobook the step names.

**Episode pattern** (*Borgen*, *Forbrydelsen*; two steps). The *viewing* step: watch the episode with Danish subtitles (Weeks 2–19) or without (Week 20 on), no pausing. The *scene work* step that follows it: take the scene that is on screen 20 minutes into the episode and watch 8 minutes from its start three times: with subtitles, without, and pausing to shadow twelve lines. Then write six sentences in Danish on the plot and one on a political or institutional term you met; 10 cards; tracker note.

**One in two** (Method 6): the episode pattern applies to the even-numbered *Borgen* episodes and to the first episode of each *Forbrydelsen* pair. The other episodes are watched straight through with no pausing and no tasks, then one tracker line (two sentences in Danish). Each step says which kind it is. The casual series (*Badehotellet*, *Klovn*, *Krøniken*) are always straight through.

**Book pattern** (set books, B2 form): every reading step names its pages; stop at the chapter break nearest the last page. Page numbers are those of the editions in Appendix K; in another edition, divide the book in the same proportions. *Intensive* sittings (the first 30 pages of each book): the audiobook of the pages first where one exists, then read them, with at most 12 words per chapter to the notebook. *Extensive* sittings (the rest): read with the audiobook where one exists, with at most 8 lookups per 30 pages. The author's lex.dk or Danish Wikipedia entry is read at the start of the book's first sitting. After the last sitting, a separate step holds the 250-word review and a 4-minute oral discussion with the AI ("discuss this book with me; ask me about the characters, the ending and what I thought"). Every reading step is followed, within the next day's steps, by its re-listen step (Listening pattern).

**Shelf rules** (SHELF books): pure input and no book pattern. Read with the audiobook if eReolen has one, at most 3–5 lookups per sitting, no cards, no review, one tracker line per sitting. The shelf books are the "other one" of the one-in-two rule for books.

**GRK pattern**: read the chapter, do the exercise book's exercises for it, check against the key, then write 20 own sentences on the chapter's points and run the Correction protocol.

**CG pattern** (two steps). The *reading* step: read the section and copy into the notebook every rule and example that was new to you. The *sentences* step: write 20 sentences on the section's points.

**UDT2 pattern**: listen to the text twice, read it, listen while reading, shadow it five times, record it, compare with the audio for stress and timing, mark the three places where you were furthest off and repeat those ten times. **Short form** (Week 20 on): listen twice, read, shadow three times; no recording and no ten-times repair loop. Shadowing is already part of the episode pattern.

**Weekly check**: the last step of every week. It passes when every step since the last check is ticked, Anki was cleared on all 7 days of the week, and the dagbog was written on at least 6 of the 7.

## Appendix A: What changes at B2

1. **No learner material.** Every source is made for Danes except the two grammars and the pronunciation texts. When something is hard, the response is more of it, not something easier.
2. **Grammar twice.** First in Danish with *Grammatikken*, so that you own the Danish terms (*bestemthed, ledsætning, sætningsadverbial, forfelt*) and can use sproget.dk without translation; then the deep pass in *A Comprehensive Grammar*. After Week 30, grammar work is error analysis only.
3. **The recurring steps carry the level.** Four Genstart a week, a long programme, two TV Avisen, four voice sessions (one of them a debate), a monologue, a retelling, a dictation, a 300-word text, four articles, a song. That is about 9 hours a week, about a third of the level, before any television or book. They are numbered steps like everything else, so none of them can be trimmed; when a week runs long, let it take more days, and never hurry the reading, series and listening steps.
4. **Literature is the point.** The Copenhagen trilogy completed, the best-selling Danish crime novel, Denmark's leading minimalist, the 1958 youth classic, the 1940 school satire, a contemporary memoir, and the poems that started a national argument. Every set book is preceded by its author's lex.dk entry and followed by a review and a discussion; that is how Danes talk about books, and you will be asked. *Skønlitteratur på P1* lets you hear critics do it. Around the set books, the shelf books are read for pleasure only, every chapter is heard again as audio, and every book with a film is followed by the film.
5. **Two spines on screen.** *Borgen* for politics and institutional Danish, *Forbrydelsen* for police, press and Copenhagen speech. *Debatten* every week is the exam for both.
6. **Subtitles end in Week 19.** After that, subtitles are a checking tool, not a viewing aid. Three exceptions keep Danish subtitles as reading input: film adaptations of books you have just read (book → film rule), the casual series (*Krøniken*, *Badehotellet*), and *Klovn: The Movie*.
7. **Speaking becomes argument.** Presentations of six minutes, debates against an assistant instructed to disagree, retelling the news aloud. The particles and discourse markers (*jo, vel, da, nok, altså, sådan, ligesom*) are now a requirement, counted at the exit.
8. **Writing becomes public.** The *læserbrev* and the *kronik* are the genres of Danish public debate. Your portfolio ends with a 500-word kronik you could send to a newspaper.
9. **Dictation from real journalism.** Zetland's audio articles are read by their authors at natural speed; the text is exact. Your error rate should fall below 5 percent.
10. **Dialects appear.** You are not learning them; you are learning to hear them and to know which is which.

## Appendix B: B2 grammar checklist

1. **Full word order model**: front field, finite verb, subject, central adverbials, non-finite verb, objects, other adverbials; the same for subordinate clauses; heavy constituents moved to the end; extraction (*sætningsknude*).
2. **Cleft and *det/der***: *Det er X, der/som ...*; *det* for clauses and infinitives; *der* in every clause type; *der* vs *det* vs *som*.
3. **Subordinate clause types** with the full subordinator set including *skønt, eftersom, idet, såfremt, medmindre, så snart, efterhånden som, hvad enten ... eller, uanset om, end ikke hvis*.
4. **Tense and aspect**: past vs perfect in narrative and reports; pluperfect for background; *er ved at, sidder/står/ligger og, er i gang med at*; future in the past; present for future and history.
5. **Modals in full**: epistemic vs deontic; *skulle* for hearsay; *må/skal* in rules; past modals as softeners and hypotheticals; *gider, behøver, tør, bør* nuances.
6. **Passive nuances**: *-s* vs *blive* vs *være* + participle; passive of verb + preposition; *få* + participle, causative and receptive; *man* as the spoken passive.
7. **Non-finite constructions**: infinitive after prepositions, perfect infinitive, participles as adjectives with agreement, *lade/få/se/høre* + bare infinitive.
8. **Reported speech and thought**: consistent backshift; *skulle*, *angiveligt*, *efter sigende*; indirect questions.
9. **Conditionals and wishes**: all types; inversion without *hvis*; *gid, bare, hvis blot*; *som om* + past.
10. **Nominal vs verbal style**: nominalisation and its formal verbs (*foretage, træffe, gennemføre, iværksætte*); unpacking into plain Danish.
11. **Determination**: generic statements; *den slags, en sådan*; definiteness with adjectives; article-less predicates and locatives.
12. **Pronoun problems**: *sin* across clauses; *de/dem der*; *hinanden*; *man/en/ens*; *nogen/nogle* in writing; *selv/sig selv*.
13. **Adjective and adverb traps**: *-t* adverbs; *dejlig/dejligt* before adjectives; participles compared with *mere/mest*.
14. **Prepositions**: the forty most common in all uses; 120 fixed combinations; *i/på/til/om/over/af/for* abstractly.
15. **Connectors and cohesion**: causal, adversative, additive, temporal, conclusive; stance adverbials (*givetvis, formentlig, tilsyneladende, angiveligt, unægtelig, ganske vist, næppe*); reference words (*dette, sidstnævnte, hertil, herved, dermed, derimod*).
16. **Punctuation in full**: comma system chosen and applied; colon, semicolon, dashes, »quotation marks«, apostrophes, abbreviations.
17. **Spelling**: compounds vs two words; *-ede/-ere*; *vidste/viste*; *sådan noget*; nationalities lower case; numbers and units in text.
18. **Register**: formal letter conventions; workplace email norms; academic markers (*således, hvorved, idet, jf.*); spoken forms (*ik', altså, ligesom, sådan*); slang and swearing recognised (*sgu, fandme, pisse-*).
19. **Modal particles, active**: *jo, vel, da, nok, nu, lige, skam, ellers, dog, mon, bare, vist, sikkert, godt*.
20. **Idioms and collocations**: 100 idioms (Appendix L of B1 plus fifty harvested); 100 verb-noun collocations (*træffe en beslutning, stille et spørgsmål, tage stilling til, lægge vægt på, gøre rede for, spille en rolle, holde øje med, drage en konklusion, komme til orde, sætte spørgsmålstegn ved*).
21. **Comparison and quantity**: *dobbelt så ... som, halvt så, tre gange så, de fleste/det meste, en tredjedel af, i gennemsnit, procent/procentpoint, knap/godt/op mod*.
22. **Numbers and measures in text**: *kr., mio., mia., pct.*, dates and times in formal writing, decades, centuries (*1800-tallet*).
23. **Word formation, productive**: prefixes *u-, mis-, van-, be-, for-, ind-, ud-, over-, under-, sam-, med-, mod-, gen-*; suffixes *-agtig, -mæssig, -værdig, -bar, -som*; *særskrivning* avoided.
24. **Discourse markers in speech**: *altså, så, jamen, nå, okay, ja men, hvad hedder det, sådan, ligesom, egentlig, faktisk, på en måde, ik'*.
25. **Varieties**: main dialect areas recognised (*jysk, fynsk, sjællandsk, bornholmsk, sønderjysk*); Copenhagen high and low; *multietnolekt*; written vs spoken norm.

## Appendix E: B2 exit reading test

**Biblioteket er mere end bøger**

Da jeg var barn, var biblioteket det eneste sted i byen, hvor man kunne være uden at skulle købe noget. Man kunne sidde i en time med en tegneserie, låne en plade, spørge bibliotekaren om alt mellem himmel og jord og gå hjem med en pose bøger, som ingen forlangte betaling for. I dag, hvor de fleste af os har adgang til hele verdens viden i lommen, er det blevet moderne at spørge, om vi overhovedet har brug for folkebiblioteket længere. Spørgsmålet er forkert stillet. Vi har mere brug for det end nogensinde, men af andre grunde end dem, vi plejer at nævne.

Det er rigtigt, at udlånet af fysiske bøger er faldet. Tallene fra Danmarks Statistik viser en nedgang på omkring en tredjedel siden årtusindskiftet, og de politikere, der gerne vil spare, peger på den kurve, hver gang budgettet skal forhandles. Men udlånstallene fortæller kun en lille del af historien. Samtidig er antallet af besøgende steget i mange kommuner, fordi bibliotekerne har fået nye funktioner: de er blevet mødesteder, læringsrum, borgerservice og i nogle byer det eneste sted med et offentligt toilet og en stikkontakt, som alle må bruge.

Den vigtigste funktion er dog stadig den, der er sværest at måle. Biblioteket er et af de sidste steder, hvor mennesker med vidt forskellige baggrunde er i samme rum uden at skulle noget bestemt. Pensionisten, der læser avis, den nytilkomne, der øver sig i dansk med en lektiehjælper, gymnasieeleven, der ikke kan koncentrere sig hjemme, og den hjemløse, der bare vil sidde varmt. De taler måske ikke sammen, men de ser hinanden, og det er ikke ligegyldigt i et samfund, hvor vi ellers i stigende grad kun møder folk, der ligner os selv.

Kritikerne indvender, at det er romantik, og at man kunne bruge pengene bedre på skoler eller ældrepleje. Det er et reelt dilemma, for kommunernes budgetter er pressede. Men når man ser på, hvad et bibliotek koster pr. borger, cirka hvad en enkelt biografbillet koster om måneden, er det svært at finde noget andet tilbud, der giver så meget for så lidt. Og de kommuner, der har lukket filialer for at spare, har som regel opdaget, at besparelsen var mindre end ventet, mens tabet for lokalsamfundet var større.

Der er også et sprogligt argument, som sjældent bliver nævnt. For de tusindvis af mennesker, der hvert år lærer dansk som voksne, er biblioteket den billigste og bedste sprogskole, der findes. Her er letlæste bøger, lydbøger, aviser og ikke mindst mennesker, man kan tale med uden at skulle betale for det. Det er svært at forestille sig en app, der kan erstatte det.

Jeg påstår ikke, at biblioteket skal se ud, som det gjorde i min barndom. Det skal udvikle sig, og det gør det allerede. Men vi skal holde op med at måle det på, hvor mange bøger der bliver lånt, og begynde at måle det på, hvad byen ville miste, hvis det ikke var der. Det svar kender de fleste af os godt. Vi skal bare huske det, næste gang nogen kalder biblioteket et levn fra fortiden.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk, hele sætninger):

1. Hvad kunne man ifølge skribenten i barndommens bibliotek, som man ikke kunne andre steder i byen?
2. Hvilket spørgsmål mener skribenten er forkert stillet?
3. Hvor meget er udlånet af fysiske bøger faldet, og siden hvornår?
4. Hvorfor er antallet af besøgende steget, selvom udlånet er faldet?
5. Hvilken funktion er ifølge skribenten den vigtigste, og hvorfor er den svær at måle?
6. Nævn to af de personer, skribenten bruger som eksempler.
7. Hvad er kritikernes indvending?
8. Hvad sammenligner skribenten bibliotekets pris med?
9. Hvad har de kommuner, der lukkede filialer, som regel opdaget?
10. Hvad foreslår skribenten, at vi måler biblioteket på i stedet for udlånstal?

**Key:** 1. Være der uden at skulle købe noget. 2. Om vi overhovedet har brug for folkebiblioteket længere. 3. Omkring en tredjedel siden årtusindskiftet. 4. Fordi bibliotekerne har fået nye funktioner (mødested, læringsrum, borgerservice, offentligt toilet, stikkontakt). 5. At mennesker med forskellige baggrunde er i samme rum uden at skulle noget bestemt; det kan ikke måles i tal. 6. Two of: pensionisten, den nytilkomne, gymnasieeleven, den hjemløse. 7. At det er romantik, og at pengene kunne bruges bedre på skoler eller ældrepleje. 8. Med prisen på en biografbillet om måneden. 9. At besparelsen var mindre end ventet, og tabet for lokalsamfundet større. 10. På hvad byen ville miste, hvis biblioteket ikke var der.
