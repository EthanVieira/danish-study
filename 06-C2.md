# Level C2: Mastery

**Prerequisite:** Milestone C1 reached (step C1-1337 ticked). The README methods still apply.

**Goal at exit:** you use Danish like an educated native speaker who reads. You can follow anything spoken in any standard or regional variety at any speed, including revue wordplay, parliamentary debate and 1940s film dialogue; read any Danish text from the medieval ballads and Holberg to this year's debut novels, with the history of the language in your ear; write in any genre, including literary prose, satire, the formal speech and the academic article, at a level an editor would print with light changes; speak for twenty minutes without notes on a specialised subject, moderate a debate, give a wedding speech, and switch register and dialect features for effect; translate between English and Danish in both directions; and proofread Danish to publication standard. Vocabulary above 18,000 words, including the older and regional vocabulary needed for the canon. There is no Danish test above Studieprøven; this level ends with a portfolio that stands in for one.

**Time:** about 910 hours over 36 weeks (33 weeks plus the three exit weeks): about 745 h of timed steps (1198 steps in all, checks included), about 125 h of Anki (30 minutes a day) and about 45 h of the *læsedagbog* (12 minutes a day). A guide week carries 15–26 h of steps, 21.5 on average: about 3.5 hours of steps a day over six days, after the dailies. Weeks 1–8 mostly carry 15–24 hours, later weeks 19.5–26, and Week 33 is the heaviest at about 26. The exit weeks carry 4.5–12.5 h of timed steps plus the exit sittings. At 2 hours a day the level takes about 76 weeks; keep the order.

**What changes at C2** (Appendix A): the canon is read from its beginning: Brandes, Holberg, Jacobsen, Pontoppidan's *Lykke-Per* in full, Johannes V. Jensen, Tom Kristensen, Martin A. Hansen, Inger Christensen complete, Andersen for adults in his own spelling, Kierkegaard, Villy Sørensen, Højholt, a contemporary novel and a work of history. Language study becomes linguistics, language history, rhetoric, translation, proofreading and pastiche. Listening reaches the archive: DRTV's *Gensyn*, radio drama, 1940s film, revues, Folketing debates. Speaking becomes performance: the lecture, the speech, the podcast. Writing becomes literature.

**How a day works at C2:** every study day starts with the two dailies, and then you do the next unticked step (and the one after it, if you have time).

- **Anki:** clear every due card, then 20 new cards a day. Most new cards are older vocabulary from the canon (with the ODS sense), collocations, revue expressions and register sets. About 30 minutes.
- **The *læsedagbog*** (the dagbog's C2 form): 150 words on what you read or heard the day before, with one stylistic observation and one sentence imitated from the text. About 12 minutes, on at least 6 days out of 7.

Nothing else recurs outside the numbered steps. The canon book, the shelf, the series, the audiobooks and radio serials, the news, the AI sessions, the recordings, the poems and the newspaper are all steps: one activity, one sitting, in the order they are to be done. Steps named "Reading: …", "Series: …" and "Listening: …" are the input of Method 6. They are Casual, and a Listening step can be done on a walk or a commute, but each one is still done in its place in the order. **Book → film:** *Hærværk*, *Sult*, *Præsten i Vejlby*, *Lykke-Per*, *Kun en pige*, *Bastarden* (after *Kaptajnen og Ann Barbara*) and *Pagten* each follow their book within a week. **One in two:** the full book pattern, with essay and seminar, applies to the canon books whose steps ask for it; the book → film novels, the neighbouring-language novels, *Mærkedage*, *Landsmænd* and the shelf are read straight through.

## Patterns

A step that names one of these patterns is done exactly as the pattern says, and it is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done. The steps say when; the patterns say how.

**Weekly check.** Each week ends with a check. It passes when every step of the week is ticked, Anki was cleared on 7 of 7 days, and the *læsedagbog* has at least 6 entries for the week. If something is missing, finish it before you start the next week.

**Listening patterns**

- ***Genstart*.** The newest episode, straight through, no pausing. One tracker line.
- ***Deadline*.** The newest broadcast on DRTV, whole. One tracker line.
- ***Klog på sprog*.** The next episode in order, starting from the first one you did not hear at B2. If DR Lyd still carries *Sprogminuttet*, play its two newest items first. Afterwards summarise the episode in three sentences and put one new term in Anki.
- **Folketing sitting.** On ft.dk, watch the first 30 minutes of the most recent *spørgetime*; in a week without one, the first 30 minutes of the most recent *forespørgselsdebat*. Then read the same passage in Folketingstidende and note five differences between what was said and what was transcribed.
- **Long programme.** The step names the programme. Take its newest episode that you have not heard; for *Den Yderste Periferi*, take the oldest archive episode you have not heard. The talk programmes (*Bogfolk*, *Skønlitteratur på P1*, *Kampen om historien*, *Magten*, *Fuld af viden*, *Brinkmanns briks*) get an eight-sentence summary; the documentaries (*P1 Dokumentar*, *Third Ear*) and *Den Yderste Periferi* get one tracker line. If a programme is no longer available, take *Third Ear*.
- **Radio drama.** One production of about an hour on DR Lyd. Odd-numbered radio dramas come from *Radioklassikeren*: the oldest episode you have not heard. Even-numbered ones come from *DR Radiodrama*: the newest production you have not heard. If one feed is empty, use the other, then *Med ørerne på stilke*; lyddramatik.dk lists what exists. Note ten words or expressions with their period.
- **Listening steps ("Listening: …").** An audiobook part or a serial episode, heard straight through with no pausing and no notes: a walk, a commute or the washing-up will do. "Part *k* of *N*" means: divide the audiobook's running time by *N* and hear the *k*th part, stopping at the nearest chapter or track break. One tracker line (title and part). If an audiobook named in a step is not on eReolen or your paid service, hear the oldest *Third Ear* episode you have not heard instead, for the same number of minutes.

**Speaking patterns**

- **AI session** (20 minutes, voice mode). There are four forms; the step says which, and gives the subject. *Seminar*: the Lecturer instruction from C2-0005. *Moderation*: the Two guests instruction; you are the host, and you keep time, summarise and press both guests. *Interview*: you are the expert; tell the assistant "Du er journalist og interviewer mig som ekspert i emnet; stil opfølgende og kritiske spørgsmål." *Small talk*: tell the assistant "Du er 25 år og fra Nørrebro. Vi småsnakker i afslappet sprog med slang; ret mig ikke undervejs." In every form, ask at the end for a list of your errors and five better phrasings, and copy them into the notebook.
- **Performance recording.** One recorded performance by the Recording protocol (README Method 3): a *foredrag* of 12 minutes without notes, a *tale* of 5 minutes for an occasion, or a podcast segment of 10 minutes. The step names the topic. Close the recording by reciting or singing the week's poem; the poem step always comes first. Then transcribe the two minutes you are least happy with, run them through the editor instruction, and say the corrected version once. A podcast segment is also trimmed, as a real episode would be.
- **Shadowing.** 15 minutes of shadowing for prosody (LRS steps 4 and 5): sentence by sentence, then live. The step names the voice: a *Deadline* host or a DR newsreader in odd weeks, the narrator of *Dickie Dick Dickens* in even weeks.
- **Dictation.** Three minutes of audio, written down by hand with as many replays as you need. The step names the source: a Weekendavisen audio article (check it against the printed article) or the week's radio drama (check it by replaying sentence by sentence at reduced speed). Count the word errors as a percentage and log it with the dictation's number.

**Writing and reading patterns**

- **Bank text.** One task from Appendix I, about 600 words unless the task says otherwise. Two passes (structure, then surface), then the editor instruction, then a clean copy with its error count filed in `writing/portfolio-C2.md`.
- **Weekendavisen.** The *Bøger* section is read whole, with one sentence in the tracker on the review you would argue with. A features sitting is the two longest features of the issue outside *Bøger*, read straight through with at most 5 lookups.
- **Daily paper.** Three articles in today's Information (or Politiken, if that is the paper you kept from C1): the leader, the longest news analysis and one piece from the culture pages. No notes.
- **Academic article.** One article of about 15 pages, read with a pencil. The step says where it comes from. Write its thesis in one sentence in the notebook.
- ***Læsedagbog* correction and card harvest.** Paste the week's entries into the editor instruction; copy each corrected sentence by hand; note the error type that came back most often. Then make ten harvested cards from the week's reading and listening: older words from the canon with their ODS sense, collocations, revue expressions.

**Poem pattern.** One item from Appendix J, learned by heart. If kalliope.org, lyrikline.org or DR has a reading or a recording of it, hear that first. Read the item aloud five times, look up every word you cannot place (ODS for the older ones), then learn it stanza by stanza until you can say or sing it without the text. You perform it at the end of the week's performance recording.

**Book pattern** (C2 form): (1) before: lex.dk, DLH, and one scholarly introduction or afterword (most Danske Klassikere and Gyldendal editions have one); this is the "before reading" step. (2) during: the reading sittings. Older texts are read with ODS open, and with the audiobook where one exists; reading is extensive, with at most 5 lookups per 30 pages, and you mark sentences to imitate. For that reason the main reads are tagged Focused or Casual; `Deep` is kept for the short texts a step names as intensive (Brandes's lecture, the study steps). Page numbers are those of the edition named in Appendix K; in another edition, divide the book in the same proportions and stop at the nearest chapter or section break. (3) after: the essay step and the seminar step. **One in two** (Method 6): part (3) applies only where the steps ask for an essay. The book → film novels (PAIRS), the neighbouring-language novels, *Mærkedage*, *Landsmænd* and the shelf are pure input: read straight through, one tracker line per sitting, at most 5 lookups a sitting.

**Essay pattern.** A 600-word essay with a thesis (not a school analysis: an argued reading), then an editor pass, then the clean copy filed in `writing/portfolio-C2.md`.

**Seminar pattern.** A 10-minute seminar in voice mode with the assistant as a literature lecturer (the Lecturer instruction), on the essay you have just written. Then write five sentences imitating sentences you marked in the book, and log the book in the tracker's Canon table.

**Shelf pattern.** A "Reading: …" step for a shelf book: borrow the book on eReolen, with the audiobook where one exists; divide it into the number of parts the step names; read the part straight through, with no cards and at most 3–5 lookups; one tracker line (title and pages).

**Archive pattern** (*Huset på Christianshavn*, films of the 1940s–1960s): no subtitles; note ten words or expressions that have changed or gone since; look each up in ODS or DDO with its date.

**Series pattern.** A "Series: …" step is one named episode without subtitles, and one tracker line. For *Huset på Christianshavn* the odd-numbered episodes get the archive pattern and the even-numbered ones are watched straight through; the step says which. For *Matador*, write down five lines worth quoting afterwards, with who says them.

## Appendix A: What changes at C2

1. **The canon from the beginning.** Ballads, *Jyske Lov*, Holberg, Brandes, Jacobsen, Pontoppidan in full, Jensen, Kristensen, Hansen, Christensen, Andersen and Kierkegaard in their own spelling, and then the present. The canon books get a scholarly introduction, an essay with a thesis, and a seminar; the novels paired with films, the neighbours' novels, the recent books and the shelf are read straight through, for volume (one in two, Method 6). By the end you can read any Danish text since 1500 without a modernised edition, and this year's novels too.
2. **Language study is linguistics and craft.** *Grammatik over det Danske Sprog* for the system, the language council's archive for norms and change, the historical dictionaries for older text, the speech archive for rhetoric, and then translation, proofreading and pastiche as tests of control.
3. **The archive, and the present.** *Huset på Christianshavn*, *Matador* without subtitles, radio drama, 1940s–60s film and Dirch Passer give you the Danish of the twentieth century as it was spoken; the revues give you wordplay at its fastest; Folketinget gives you public speech at length, with the transcript to check against. A series is always running, and the last one (*Når støvet har lagt sig*), the recent films (*Bastarden*, *Pagten*) and the shelf keep the Danish of now in your ear.
4. **Performance.** Twelve-minute lectures without notes, occasional speeches in the Danish tradition (the *tale* is a genre Danes are judged on), podcast segments, moderation, pastiche read aloud. Fluency is assumed; the target is command.
5. **Literature as output.** The *novelle*, the essay in the tradition of Villy Sørensen and Suzanne Brøgger, the revue monologue, the kronik on language. The editor instruction's verdict "publishable with light edits" is the standard.
6. **Translation both ways** against printed translations. Nothing shows the gap between languages as exactly.
7. **The neighbours in the original.** A Norwegian and a Swedish novel, read without a dictionary. Receptive command of the three Scandinavian languages is part of what an educated Dane has.
8. **The stx exam.** The written Danish exam that Danish eighteen-year-olds sit is the nearest thing to a C2 test; two full sets under time (real sets where you can get them, otherwise sets built to the ministry's published specification), marked by the ministry's own grade descriptions, at a grade a Dane would be pleased with.
9. **Portfolio, not certificate.** No institution issues a C2 in Danish. The exit check is designed so that every claim in the CEFR C2 descriptors is backed by a recording or a document you could show.
10. **Afterwards.** Ten cards a day, the reading diary, one book a month, and the habit of noticing. The level does not end; it becomes how you live in the language.

## Appendix B: C2 language checklist

1. **Information structure**: front-field choice explained for every sentence; focus and theme; heavy constituents; the *sætningsknude*; the *der*-construction's exact conditions.
2. **Modal particles**: all fourteen in their GDS senses, combined (*jo nok, da vel, nu nok, skam ikke*), with the right stress and intonation.
3. **Determination in depth**: generic and specific reference, bare nouns, definiteness with modifiers, proper-name usage, the definite suffix versus the article for emphasis.
4. **Complementation**: which verbs take *at*-clause, *om*-clause, infinitive, bare infinitive, *hv*-clause, and with what meaning difference.
5. **Aspect and tense in narrative art**: the historical present, free indirect style (*dækket direkte tale*) and its tense logic, the pluperfect as memory.
6. **Passives and impersonals in every register**, including the archaic (*vorder*), the administrative and the literary.
7. **Older morphology, receptive**: plural verb forms, *-e* infinitives, the genitive in *-s* on phrases, *thi, hvilken, saaledes, tilforn*, pre-1948 orthography read at sight, eighteenth-century orthography with a crib.
8. **Language history**: the periods, the sound changes, the 1948 reform, the 2024 changes, and the ability to date a text by its language within twenty years.
9. **Dialect and sociolect**: features of each main dialect, Copenhagen high and low, *multietnolekt*, and the ability to imitate two of them recognisably.
10. **Neighbouring languages**: Norwegian and Swedish read as novels; Nynorsk and older Swedish recognised.
11. **Rhetoric**: the classical apparatus in Danish terms; the conventions of every Danish occasional speech; moderation; the New Year address; the revue monologue.
12. **Humour**: pun mechanisms, Storm P.'s logic, ATS conventions, the *underdrivelse* as a national register, revue satire.
13. **Register at will**, including deliberate breach for effect, archaism for irony, and the *kancellistil* as parody.
14. **Genre command**: novelle, essay, kronik, leder, anmeldelse, nekrolog, tale, portræt, reportage, høringssvar, notat, abstract, subtitle, translator's note.
15. **Style analysis and pastiche**: the ability to name an author from a page and to write a page that names itself.
16. **Translation**: fidelity, idiom, cadence; subtitle norms; the systematic asymmetries between Danish and English (compression, compounds, particles, tense).
17. **Proofreading**: the marks, the rules, the eye; 40 errors in 1500 words caught with at most one miss.
18. **Editing**: structure, cutting, sharpening a thesis, killing nominalisation, cadence.
19. **Idiom and quotation**: 300 idioms; the *bevingede ord* Danes quote (Grundtvig, Kierkegaard, Storm P., PH, Matador, Olsen-banden); proverbs.
20. **Older and regional vocabulary**: 500 items from the canon with their ODS senses; 200 dialect items.
21. **Prosody**: newsreader, lecturer, conversational and revue deliveries distinguishable on tape; stød and stress native-like in every register.
22. **Discourse in speech**: moderation phrases, turn management in a three-way debate, the art of the *indskudt bemærkning*, backchannels in the right dialect.
23. **Names of things, complete**: institutions, offices, laws, historical events, literary periods, the canon's titles and dates.
24. **Self-editing**: every text two passes; every recording listened to once; the repair list maintained.
25. **Judgement**: knowing which Danish to use for which Dane, and when to say less.

## Appendix E: C2 exit reading test

**Sproget som arv**

Der er en bestemt slags dansk, som ingen længere taler, men som alle, der har gået i skole her, bærer rundt på. Det er det sprog, der ligger i salmebogen, i *Jyske Lov*s fortale, i Grundtvigs sange, i Holbergs komedier og i de replikker fra *Matador*, som man kan høre citeret ved et hvilket som helst middagsselskab, hvis man venter længe nok. Det er ikke et sprog, man lærer; det er et sprog, man arver, og som de fleste arvinger er vi ikke særlig opmærksomme på, hvad vi har fået.

Det opdager man først, når nogen mangler det. Jeg havde for nogle år siden en kollega, som var kommet til landet som voksen og talte et dansk, der i enhver målbar henseende var bedre end mit: mere korrekt, mere præcist, renset for de småfejl og dialektrester, som jeg selv slæber rundt på. Alligevel var der noget, der ikke var der. Når nogen ved frokostbordet sagde "det var så lidt", og en anden svarede "ja, det var det", og alle grinede, forstod han ordene, men ikke latteren. Han vidste ikke, at man i det øjeblik citerede en replik, som hele bordet havde hørt som børn, og at latteren ikke gjaldt vitsen, men genkendelsen.

Man kunne kalde det en uretfærdighed, og det er det på en måde også. Sproget som arv er en form for kapital, der uddeles efter fødsel og skolegang og ikke efter fortjeneste. Men det er samtidig den eneste kapital, der bliver større af at blive delt. Hver gang min kollega spurgte, hvad der var så morsomt, og fik svaret, blev han en smule rigere, og det gjorde vi andre også, for det er umuligt at forklare, hvorfor "det var så lidt" er sjovt, uden at komme til at tænke over, hvad det egentlig er, vi har fået overleveret, og af hvem.

Det er her, litteraturhistorien får en betydning, som den sjældent tillægges. Man læser ikke Holberg for at få at vide, hvad der skete i 1723, og heller ikke, med fuld respekt, fordi *Erasmus Montanus* er verdens bedste komedie. Man læser ham, fordi den måde, hvorpå danskere i tre hundrede år har grinet ad den, der har læst for meget og forstået for lidt, stadig er den måde, vi griner på. Per Degn lever i enhver folketingsdebat. Jeppe lever i enhver diskussion om, hvem der skal have skylden. Det er ikke litteraturhistorie; det er en brugsanvisning til det samfund, man lever i.

Jeg vil derfor tillade mig at foreslå noget, som lyder gammeldags: at den, der for alvor vil lære dansk, ikke skal nøjes med det dansk, der tales, men også skal tilegne sig det dansk, der citeres. Det er et større arbejde, og det er ikke det, sprogskolerne har tid til. Men det er den eneste vej ind i latteren ved frokostbordet, og latteren er, når alt kommer til alt, den mest pålidelige prøve på, om man er kommet hjem i et sprog.

Min kollega bestod den prøve efter nogle år. Det skete en dag, hvor en af os kom for sent til et møde og undskyldte sig med, at toget havde været forsinket, og han, uden at se op fra sine papirer, sagde: "Ja, ja, Larsen, det siger De hver gang." Vi grinede, og han grinede, og ingen af os behøvede at forklare noget. Det var så lidt.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk; 7–10 kræver fortolkning):

1. Hvad forstår skribenten ved "sproget som arv"?
2. Hvordan var kollegaens dansk, målt objektivt?
3. Hvad manglede kollegaen ved frokostbordet, og hvad gjaldt latteren?
4. Hvorfor kalder skribenten arven en uretfærdighed, og hvad er modargumentet?
5. Hvad er ifølge skribenten den egentlige grund til at læse Holberg?
6. Hvad foreslår skribenten, at den, der vil lære dansk, skal gøre ud over at lære det talte sprog?
7. Hvad er tekstens genre, og hvilke træk viser det?
8. Forklar de to *Matador*-citater ("det var så lidt" og "det siger De hver gang") og deres funktion i teksten.
9. Hvordan bruger skribenten Per Degn og Jeppe? Hvad er det for en type argument?
10. Hvad betyder den sidste sætning, og hvorfor er den effektiv som slutning?

**Key:** 1. Det citerede, fælles dansk fra salmebog, lov, Grundtvig, Holberg og Matador, som man får gennem opvækst og skole, ikke gennem undervisning. 2. Bedre end skribentens: mere korrekt og præcist, uden småfejl og dialekt. 3. Genkendelsen af citater; latteren gjaldt genkendelsen, ikke vitsen. 4. Fordi den uddeles efter fødsel og skolegang, ikke fortjeneste; modargumentet er, at den vokser ved at blive delt. 5. Fordi Holbergs måde at grine ad den halvlærde stadig er danskernes måde; komedierne er en brugsanvisning til samfundet. 6. Også at tilegne sig det dansk, der citeres. 7. Et personligt essay: jeg-fortæller, anekdote, generalisering, forslag, ringslutning, let ironi. 8. Replikker fra *Matador*, som danskere citerer; i teksten viser de først kollegaens udenforskab og til sidst hans optagelse; slutreplikken gentager åbningen. 9. Som eksempler på, at litterære typer stadig strukturerer nutidens adfærd; et argument ved analogi og ved *perspektivering*. 10. "Det var så lidt" er både Matador-citatet, en beskeden bemærkning om, hvor lidt der skulle til, og en ironisk underdrivelse af, hvor stort det var; slutningen lukker ringen og lader læseren selv høre latteren.
