# Level C1: Effective Operational Proficiency

**Prerequisite:** Milestone B2 reached (step B2-1327 ticked). The README methods still apply; the recurring activities continue in the C1 forms defined under Patterns below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow lectures, debates, satire and fast group conversation without effort; read literary prose of any period from Blixen and Bang to this year's debut novel, and academic and journalistic Danish with full understanding of tone, irony and register; speak fluently and precisely on abstract and specialised topics, adjusting register at will; and write long, well-structured texts (an academic essay with sources, a 2000-word *kronik*, a literary analysis, a formal *høringssvar*) with only occasional slips. Vocabulary about 12,000 words. You understand written Norwegian and Swedish for practical purposes, recognise the main Danish dialects and sociolects, and can read texts in the pre-1948 orthography. This is the level of Studieprøven, the test for admission to Danish higher education.

**Time:** about 955 hours over 36 weeks: about 800 hours of steps (1337 steps; their time tags plus the milestone and exit checks) and about 155 hours of dailies (Anki at 20 new cards a day on a deck of 10,000+ cards is 25–30 minutes a day; the *læsedagbog* is about 10 minutes a day; both every day). A typical guide week is about 22 hours of steps. That is roughly four and a half hours a day, six days a week, dailies included. At 2.5 hours a day the level takes about 64 weeks; keep the order.

**What changes at C1** (Appendix A): the level is organised around literature and public intellectual life. Ten books from the canon and the present, each read as a Dane with a gymnasium education would read it: with the author's biography, a literary-history context and an analysis afterwards. Language study turns to style, rhetoric, language history and neighbouring languages. Listening moves to *Deadline*, P1 analysis and lectures. Writing reaches the academic essay and the full-length *kronik*. Grammar as such is finished; what remains is precision.

**How a day works at C1** (README, "A daily session"): first the two dailies. Clear Anki and learn the day's 20 new cards. Write the *læsedagbog*, the C1 form of the dagbog: 100 words on what you read that day, with one quotation and one observation about the language. Then do the next unticked step, and the one after it if you have time. Everything else in the level is a numbered step, done in order: every reading sitting, both of the canon books (book pattern, with analysis) and of the free-reading shelf of nine contemporary novels (SHELF, read for pleasure with no analysis); every series episode, both of the Deep series that carries the episode pattern and of the Casual series beside it (SERIES, one log line per episode); every listening item, from *Genstart* and *Deadline* to *Sara & Monopolet*, *Third Ear*, the documentary series and the album of the month (LISTEN); and every voice session, talk, dictation, article, text and poem. Nothing is left for you to schedule. The one-in-two rule (README Method 6) works here by track: the canon books and the Deep series get the full pattern, the shelf and the Casual series are pure input.

## Patterns

Every recurring activity at C1 is defined once here. A step that names a pattern is done exactly as the pattern says, and it is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done. The steps fix what you do and in which order; nothing below asks you to plan a week.

**The dailies.** (1) Anki: clear every due card, then add and learn 20 new cards. The new cards come from the previous day's steps, taken in this order until you have 20: cards a step told you to make (the episode pattern, RO, the sweeps); the pencil-marked sentences from the canon book; the collocations and idioms you underlined in the press sittings. If the day gave fewer than 20, add what there is. Most cards are collocations, connectors and stylistic alternatives (C1-0005). (2) The *læsedagbog*: every day, 100 words in `writing/laesedagbog.md` on what you read that day in the steps, with one quotation and one observation about the language. Weekly checks ask for Anki every day (the exit check tolerates 6 missed days over the whole level) and the *læsedagbog* on at least 6 of every 7 days.

**News-podcast pattern** (*Genstart*, *P1 Orientering*): the newest episode you have not heard, once through without pausing; then a note in Danish in the notebook: three sentences for a *Genstart* episode, six for the first hour of *P1 Orientering*, which stands in for both *Genstart* episodes in every fourth week.

**Neighbour-listening pattern** (Weeks 21–26): the newest episode of *Forklart* (Aftenposten's daily news explainer, in Norwegian) or of *P3 Dokumentär* (Sveriges Radio, in Swedish), once through without pausing; then one line in Danish in the tracker saying what it was about. In these six weeks the two programmes take the place of the two *Genstart* episodes.

**Deadline pattern**: the newest edition of *Deadline* on DRTV (DR's news analysis programme, Monday to Thursday, about 30 minutes), no subtitles; then a 5-sentence Danish summary of the main interview's argument in the notebook.

**Long-programme pattern**: the step names the programme; through the level they come in the order *Supertanker*, *K-Live*, *Magten*, *Skønlitteratur på P1* or *Bogfolk* by turns, a *Fuld af viden* lecture. Hear the newest episode you have not heard, once through; then write an 8-sentence summary: each participant's position, the host's framing, the conclusion. If the named programme has ended, use the newest episode in its archive that you have not heard; if the archive is gone as well, take the next programme in the order. The *Tænkepauser* steps are separate and never replace the long programme.

**Listening pattern** (Casual; a walk or a commute is fine): hear the item once and write one tracker line (programme, episode, one sentence).
- *Sara & Monopolet* (DR P4): the newest episode; its first dilemma block, about 40 minutes.
- *Third Ear*: the newest documentary you have not heard; when there is nothing new, work backwards through the archive from the newest.
- The DR Lyd documentary series of the month: nine series, each for four weeks. In the step that opens a month, go to DR Lyd's documentary category and take the first series listed that has at least four episodes and that you have not heard; then one episode per step, in order. If a series runs out before its month does, start the next one by the same rule.
- The album of the month: the nine albums listed in Appendix K, in that order, each heard once a week for four weeks. The first listen is with the lyrics open, read once; the other three are plain listening.

**Voice-session pattern**: 20 minutes in AI voice mode with the Seminar instruction (C1-0005): the assistant is a fellow student or a lecturer. The step names the topic. Open with a claim about it in two sentences; the assistant challenges your claims and asks for evidence. End by asking for the error list (word choice and style, not only grammar) and copy three corrections into the notebook. Keep the transcript: the milestones read them.

**Talk pattern**: Recording protocol (README Method 3). Start the recording by reciting or singing the week's poem or song from memory; then speak for 8 minutes on the Appendix F part 2 topic the step names. Afterwards transcribe the talk (correct an automatic transcript by ear) and run it through the Correction protocol (Method 4).

**Reading-aloud pattern**: 3 minutes of literary prose, recorded. The step names the book: open it at the page where your most recent `Reading:` step in it began and read aloud from there. Then, where an audiobook exists, play the same passage in the audiobook and note three places where its pace or stress differs from yours; one tracker line.

**Dictation pattern**: the first two minutes of the audio version of the Weekendavisen article you read in the sitting just before (if that article has no audio version, the newest Zetland audio article). Write what you hear, pausing as you need; compare with the text; log the word-error percentage in the tracker.

**Writing-bank pattern**: the Appendix I task the step names: 400 words unless the task gives another length; then the Editor instruction (C1-0005); correct; file the clean copy with its error count in `writing/portfolio-C1.md`. "Again" means the task is written afresh and then compared with your first version in two sentences.

**Press pattern**: one pass, at most 3 lookups, no notes; underline two collocations or idioms for the next day's Anki cards.
- *Weekendavisen sitting*: one long article from the current issue. Sitting 1 is the lead article of the *Samfund* section, sitting 2 the lead review of *Bøger*, sitting 3 the lead article of *Ideer*. If you have already read it, take the next article in the same section.
- *Newspaper sitting*: one article from today's issue of your daily paper (Information or Politiken); the step names which article.
- *videnskab.dk*: the two newest articles on the front page.

**Læsedagbog-correction pattern**: paste the entries written since the last correction into the AI with the Correction protocol (README Method 4); read every correction; copy the three most instructive corrected sentences into the notebook by hand.

**Poem pattern**: the Appendix J item the step names. First listen to the poet's own reading if lyrikline.org or DR's archive has one (for a song: the original recording, twice). Then learn it by heart. Done when you can say or sing it twice without the text. You recite it at the start of the week's talk recording.

**Book pattern** (the canon books; C1 form). It has three kinds of step:
1. *Before reading* (a `Culture` step where the book has one): the author's entry on lex.dk and the relevant DLH chapter.
2. *Reading sittings* (`Reading: <title>, part k of N`): before the first sitting, divide the book's pages into N equal parts and write the page ranges in the tracker; each sitting reads one part. Where an audiobook exists, begin the sitting by listening to the opening chapter of the part (or its first ten minutes), then read the part from its beginning. Read intensively for the first 20 pages of the book, extensively after that with at most 6 lookups per 30 pages, marking in pencil every sentence whose construction you could not have produced. One tracker line per sitting.
3. *After the book* (a `Produce` step): a 400-word analysis in the gymnasium format (*resumé, fortæller og synsvinkel, komposition, sproglige virkemidler, tema, perspektivering*), corrected with the Editor instruction; then a 6-minute AI seminar on the book; then the last five sentences you marked, copied into the notebook and imitated with your own content. Fill in the book's row in the Canon table.

**Episode pattern** (C1 form, for the Deep series only: *Herrens veje*, *Arvingerne*, *Riget*, *1864*). Each episode is two steps. *Viewing*: the whole episode, no subtitles. *Write-up*: rewatch the 10-minute stretch you understood least, with subtitles, as a check only; then eight sentences on the plot and two on language (an idiom, a register shift, a dialect feature); 10 cards.

**Series pattern** (the Casual series): the named episode, no subtitles, no notes; one tracker line.

**Free-reading pattern** (the shelf): ebook or paper, with the eReolen audiobook as read-along where one exists; no pencil, no cards, at most 3 lookups per sitting; one tracker line per sitting (title, pages). Before a book's first sitting, divide its pages into the number of parts the steps name and write the page ranges in the tracker; each `Reading:` step reads one part. The time tags assume you read for the story at the audiobook's pace or faster; a step is done when its part is read, so let a sitting run on if the part needs it.

**Tænkepauser pattern**: the step names the volume: the first thirteen of the series, in order. Read the book in one sitting (about 60 pages); then listen to the *Tænkepauser* podcast conversation with its author; one tracker line. If a volume is out of print and not on eReolen, take the lowest-numbered volume you have not read.

**Language-study pattern**: read the section named; write every rule you did not already apply into the notebook in Danish; then apply it to your three most recent portfolio texts and note what changed.

## Appendix A: What changes at C1

1. **Reading like a graduate.** Every canon book comes with its author's biography, its place in *Dansk litteraturs historie*, and an analysis in the gymnasium format afterwards. That is the shared framework through which educated Danes discuss literature; owning it lets you join the conversation. Beside the canon, the free-reading shelf is read the way Danes read on the train: for the story, with no analysis at all. Both kinds of reading are needed.
2. **Language study is style.** Grammar is done. *Håndbog i Nudansk* and Retskrivningsreglerne make your writing correct to the letter; the gymnasium handbook gives you the vocabulary of analysis and rhetoric; the language-history weeks let you read anything written since 1700.
3. **Listening is analysis.** *Deadline* once a week and a long P1 programme, each summarised as an argument, not as facts. Lectures and *Tænkepauser* audiobooks train the long attention span a university demands. Around them, the Listening steps carry conversation, documentary and song, so that news is no longer most of what you hear.
4. **Speaking is seminar.** The assistant is now a fellow student who disagrees and asks for evidence, or an examiner who asks you to nuance and perspectivise. Register control is the skill: academic in the seminar, colloquial in the reflections, and the ability to switch mid-sentence.
5. **Writing is public and academic.** The bank moves to 400 words; the milestones demand analyses and an academic essay with sources; the level ends with a 2000-word kronik written to Politiken's length and a *høringssvar* on a real consultation.
6. **The neighbours.** Six weeks give you receptive Norwegian and Swedish, which every educated Dane has and which the Nordic labour market assumes. *Broen* is the test.
7. **Varieties and history.** Dialects, sociolects, the 1886 spelling of Bang, Kierkegaard's 1843 prose, Holberg's eighteenth-century Danish, all on the Royal Library's tekster.kb.dk. You are not asked to produce any of it; you are asked to read and hear it without stumbling.
8. **Poems by heart.** Twenty-four poems from Kingo to Ravn and twelve songs, one item a week, recited or sung on tape. Nothing lodges the cadence and the older vocabulary of Danish more surely.
9. **Studieprøven is the model exam.** Its reading, writing and oral formats shape the last eight weeks, and the exit check uses its example papers and assessment guides.
10. **Time.** About four and a half hours a day, six days a week, the two dailies included. The recurring steps (listening, voice sessions, talk, writing, the press, the poem) come to about eleven hours a week, and the free-reading shelf and the Casual series add about three more. If life intervenes, the level stretches; the order does not change.

## Appendix B: C1 language checklist

1. **Sentence architecture**: control of length and cadence; fronting for cohesion; heavy constituents last; extraction; no more than one subordinate level deeper than a native writer would use.
2. **Verbal vs nominal style**: nominalisation used deliberately in formal prose and avoided elsewhere; the formal support verbs (*foretage, træffe, gennemføre, iværksætte, afgive, indgive, fremsætte*).
3. **Modality and hedging**: *synes at, kan tyde på, peger i retning af, formentlig, givetvis, næppe, skulle efter forlydende*; epistemic *må/kan/skulle* in academic prose.
4. **Tense in academic and literary prose**: historical present; reported past; the *plusquamperfektum* for background; the present perfect in abstracts.
5. **Passives and agentless constructions** in report style: *-s* passive, *der*-constructions, *man*, *blive*; when to name the agent.
6. **Connectors in full**: *dermed, følgelig, imidlertid, hvorimod, såvel ... som, dels ... dels, i det omfang, for så vidt, med henblik på, med hensyn til, i forlængelse af, i lyset af, på baggrund af*.
7. **Relative and participial compression**: *hvorved, hvorefter, hvortil, hvis, hvilket*; present and past participles as attributive clauses (*den af udvalget foreslåede ændring*).
8. **Idiom, collocation, metaphor**: 200 idioms and 200 collocations; conventional metaphors of Danish public debate (*at trække i land, at sætte sig mellem to stole, at gå over åen efter vand, at sluge en kamel, at være ude i tovene*).
9. **Register at will**: academic, journalistic, administrative, literary, spoken-formal, spoken-casual; the markers of each; switching mid-text for effect.
10. **Irony and understatement**: recognising and producing the Danish *underdrivelse* (*ikke helt dårligt, ganske udmærket, det kunne have været værre*) and irony markers (*skam, sådan set, vist, unægtelig*).
11. **Punctuation to the rules**: Retskrivningsreglerne's comma paragraphs; the chosen comma system; quotation, dashes, ellipses, brackets, semicolons used as Danish editors use them.
12. **Orthography to the rules**: compounds, capitals, apostrophes, hyphens, loanword inflection (*jobbet, teamet, websites/websider*), the 2024 changes.
13. **Old orthography, receptive**: pre-1948 conventions; nineteenth-century inflection; Holberg-era vocabulary with a crib.
14. **Neighbouring languages, receptive**: Bokmål and Swedish read and heard for gist and detail; Nynorsk recognised.
15. **Varieties, receptive**: the main dialects, Copenhagen high and low, *multietnolekt*; the features that mark each.

## Appendix E: C1 exit reading test

**Om at læse Blixen i dag**

Der findes forfattere, man vokser fra, og forfattere, man vokser ind i. Karen Blixen hører til de sidste, og det er ikke nogen selvfølge. Da jeg første gang forsøgte mig med *Vinter-Eventyr* som gymnasieelev, oplevede jeg sproget som en mur: de lange, snørklede perioder, de gammeldags ordformer, den insisterende brug af store bogstaver i en tid, hvor alle andre for længst havde lagt dem fra sig. Jeg gav op efter tredive sider og kaldte hende, med den sikkerhed man kun har som syttenårig, for utidssvarende. Det var først tyve år senere, da jeg af tilfældige grunde kom til at læse *Sorg-Agre* højt for en syg ven, at det gik op for mig, hvad jeg var gået glip af.

Det, der ved første møde virker som en manér, viser sig ved nærmere bekendtskab at være en metode. Blixen skriver ikke gammeldags, fordi hun ikke kan andet; hun skriver sådan, fordi hendes fortællinger handler om mennesker, der lever under en anden orden end vores, og fordi sproget skal bære den orden med sig. Den gamle godsejer i *Sorg-Agre*, som lader en bondekone høste en hel mark på én dag for at redde sin søn, er ikke en skurk, selvom en nutidig læser uvægerligt oplever ham sådan i begyndelsen. Han handler efter en lov, som han selv er underlagt, og fortællingens skandale er ikke hans hårdhed, men at han har ret på sine egne præmisser. Det er en tanke, der er svær at rumme for os, som er opdraget til at mene, at enhver orden, der koster menneskeliv, dermed har diskvalificeret sig selv. Blixen tvinger os til at holde tanken i luften lidt længere, end vi bryder os om.

Man kan indvende, at det er en aristokratisk fantasi, og indvendingen er ikke uden vægt. Blixen var baronesse, hun levede af en farm, der byggede på et kolonialt system, og hendes forestillinger om skæbne og storhed bærer tydelige spor af det. Men det er en fattig læsning, der standser der. Det interessante er ikke, at hun forsvarer en gammel verden, for det gør hun strengt taget ikke; det interessante er, at hun beskriver, hvad det koster at leve i en verden, hvor den gamle orden er brudt sammen, og ingen ny er trådt i stedet. Det er, når alt kommer til alt, også vores situation.

Dertil kommer sproget, som jeg altså engang tog for en mur. Læser man Blixen højt, hvad man bør, opdager man, at periodernes længde ikke er pynt, men rytme. Sætningerne er bygget til at blive sagt, og de har en musik, som moderne dansk prosa, med sine korte, kontante sætninger, sjældent tillader sig. Der er en grund til, at hendes tekster egner sig så godt til oplæsning, og til at Gabriel Axel kunne filme *Babettes Gæstebud* næsten replik for replik.

Det er ikke ensbetydende med, at alt hos Blixen er lige godt, eller at hun bør læses ukritisk. Nogle af fortællingerne er for lange, nogle af pointerne for demonstrative, og den kultur, der voksede op omkring hende på Rungstedlund i hendes sidste år, gjorde hende ikke nogen tjeneste. Men det er en forfatter, hvis bøger bliver bedre af, at man bliver ældre, og det kan ikke siges om ret mange. Måske skulle vi holde op med at give hende til syttenårige og i stedet give hende til fyrreårige, som har mistet noget og har brug for et sprog for tabet.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk, hele sætninger; spørgsmål 8–10 kræver fortolkning):

1. Hvad var skribentens første oplevelse af Blixens sprog, og hvor gammel var skribenten?

**Key:** 1. Sproget virkede som en mur (lange perioder, gammeldags former, store bogstaver); skribenten var sytten. 2. At læse *Sorg-Agre* højt for en syg ven, tyve år senere. 3.
