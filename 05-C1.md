# Level C1: Effective Operational Proficiency

**Prerequisite:** Milestone B2 reached (step B2-372 ticked). The README methods still apply; the weekly rhythm continues in the C1 form below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow lectures, debates, satire and fast group conversation without effort; read literary prose of any period from Blixen and Bang to this year's debut novel, and academic and journalistic Danish with full understanding of tone, irony and register; speak fluently and precisely on abstract and specialised topics, adjusting register at will; and write long, well-structured texts (an academic essay with sources, a 2000-word *kronik*, a literary analysis, a formal *høringssvar*) with only occasional slips. Vocabulary about 12,000 words. You understand written Norwegian and Swedish for practical purposes, recognise the main Danish dialects and sociolects, and can read texts in the pre-1948 orthography. This is the level of Studieprøven, the test for admission to Danish higher education.

**Time:** about 640 hours over 36 weeks, roughly 2.5 to 3 hours a day, 6 days a week. At 2 hours a day the level takes about 50 weeks; keep the order.

**What changes at C1** (Appendix A): the level is organised around literature and public intellectual life. Ten books from the canon and the present, each read as a Dane with a gymnasium education would read it: with the author's biography, a literary-history context and an analysis afterwards. Language study turns to style, rhetoric, language history and neighbouring languages. Listening moves to *Deadline*, P1 analysis and lectures. Writing reaches the academic essay and the full-length *kronik*. Grammar as such is finished; what remains is precision.

## The C1 weekly rhythm

- **R1 Listening** (about 240 min): *Genstart* four episodes (or *P1 Orientering*'s first hour twice instead of two of them), each with a 3-sentence note; *Deadline* twice (DR's news analysis programme, Monday to Thursday, about 30 minutes, on DRTV), each followed by a 5-sentence Danish summary of the main interview's argument; one long programme a week in rotation (*Supertanker*, *K-Live*, *Magten*, *Skønlitteratur på P1* or *Bogfolk*, a *Fuld af viden* lecture) with an 8-sentence summary; and every second week the *Tænkepauser* item (the audiobook if you bought it, otherwise the book read in one sitting plus the podcast conversation with its author) instead of the long programme.
- **R2 Speaking** (about 130 min): four 20-minute AI sessions in seminar mode (the assistant is a fellow student or a lecturer; topics from the week's reading; it challenges your claims and asks for evidence); one recorded talk of 8 minutes on the week's topic (Appendix F part 2), transcribed and corrected; one 3-minute reading aloud of a literary passage, compared with the audiobook where one exists; one 2-minute dictation from a Weekendavisen or Zetland audio article, logged.
- **R3 Writing and reading** (about 200 min): a *læsedagbog* entry of 100 words on five days (what you read, one quotation, one observation about the language); one writing-bank text (Appendix I) of 400 words, corrected and filed in `writing/portfolio-C1.md`; Weekendavisen: three articles a week (one from the *Bøger* section); a daily newspaper: two articles a day, five days; videnskab.dk: two articles a week; ten harvested collocation or idiom cards.
- **R4 Poem or song** (about 40 min): one item from Appendix J, learned by heart and recited or sung on the recording for the week.

Weekly checks require all of the above plus the content steps.

**Book pattern** (C1 form): (1) before: the author's entry on lex.dk and the relevant DLH chapter; (2) during: audiobook chapter first where one exists; intensive on the first 20 pages, extensive after that with at most 6 lookups per 30 pages, marking in pencil every sentence whose construction you could not have produced; (3) after: a 400-word analysis in the gymnasium format (*resumé, fortæller og synsvinkel, komposition, sproglige virkemidler, tema, perspektivering*), corrected; a 6-minute AI seminar on the book; the five marked sentences copied into the notebook and imitated with your own content.

**Episode pattern** (C1 form): no subtitles; one 10-minute scene rewatched with subtitles as a check only; eight sentences on the plot and two on language (an idiom, a register shift, a dialect feature); 10 cards.

**Language-study pattern**: read the section named; write every rule you did not already apply into the notebook in Danish; then apply it to three of your own portfolio texts and note what changed.

## Appendix A: What changes at C1

1. **Reading like a graduate.** Every book comes with its author's biography, its place in *Dansk litteraturs historie*, and an analysis in the gymnasium format afterwards. That is the shared framework through which educated Danes discuss literature; owning it lets you join the conversation.
2. **Language study is style.** Grammar is done. *Håndbog i Nudansk* and Retskrivningsreglerne make your writing correct to the letter; the gymnasium handbook gives you the vocabulary of analysis and rhetoric; the language-history weeks let you read anything written since 1700.
3. **Listening is analysis.** *Deadline* twice a week and a P1 programme, each summarised as an argument, not as facts. Lectures and *Tænkepauser* audiobooks train the long attention span a university demands.
4. **Speaking is seminar.** The assistant is now a fellow student who disagrees and asks for evidence, or an examiner who asks you to nuance and perspectivise. Register control is the skill: academic in the seminar, colloquial in the reflections, and the ability to switch mid-sentence.
5. **Writing is public and academic.** The bank moves to 400 words; the milestones demand analyses and an academic essay with sources; the level ends with a 2000-word kronik written to Politiken's length and a *høringssvar* on a real consultation.
6. **The neighbours.** Six weeks give you receptive Norwegian and Swedish, which every educated Dane has and which the Nordic labour market assumes. *Broen* is the test.
7. **Varieties and history.** Dialects, sociolects, the 1886 spelling of Bang, Kierkegaard's 1843 prose, Holberg's eighteenth-century Danish, all on the Royal Library's tekster.kb.dk. You are not asked to produce any of it; you are asked to read and hear it without stumbling.
8. **Poems by heart.** Twenty-four poems from Kingo to Ravn, one a week, recited on tape. Nothing lodges the rhythm and the older vocabulary of Danish more surely.
9. **Studieprøven is the model exam.** Its reading, writing and oral formats shape the last eight weeks, and the exit check uses its example papers and assessment guides.
10. **Time.** Two and a half hours a day. The rhythm alone is ten hours a week. If life intervenes, the level stretches; the order does not change.

## Appendix B: C1 language checklist

1. **Sentence architecture**: control of length and rhythm; fronting for cohesion; heavy constituents last; extraction; no more than one subordinate level deeper than a native writer would use.
2. **Verbal vs nominal style**: nominalisation used deliberately in formal prose and avoided elsewhere; the formal support verbs (*foretage, træffe, gennemføre, iværksætte, afgive, indgive, fremsætte*).
3. **Modality and hedging**: *synes at, kan tyde på, peger i retning af, formentlig, givetvis, næppe, skulle efter forlydende*; epistemic *må/kan/skulle* in academic prose.
4. **Tense in academic and literary prose**: historical present; reported past; the *plusquamperfektum* for background; the present perfect in abstracts.
5. **Passives and agentless constructions** in report style: *-s* passive, *der*-constructions, *man*, *blive*; when to name the agent.
6. **Connectors in full**: *dermed, følgelig, imidlertid, hvorimod, såvel ... som, dels ... dels, i det omfang, for så vidt, med henblik på, med hensyn til, i forlængelse af, i lyset af, på baggrund af*.
7. **Relative and participial compression**: *hvorved, hvorefter, hvortil, hvis, hvilket*; present and past participles as attributive clauses (*den af udvalget foreslåede ændring*).
8. **Idiom, collocation, metaphor**: 200 idioms and 200 collocations; conventional metaphors of Danish public debate (*at trække i land, at sætte sig mellem to stole, at gå over åen efter vand, at sluge en kamel, at være ude i tovene*).
9. **Register at will**: academic, journalistic, administrative, literary, spoken-formal, spoken-casual; the markers of each; switching mid-text for effect.
10. **Irony and understatement**: recognising and producing the Danish *underdrivelse* (*ikke helt dårligt, ganske udmærket, det kunne have været værre*) and irony markers (*skam, sådan set, vist, unægtelig*).
11. **Punctuation to the rules**: Retskrivningsreglerne's comma paragraphs; the chosen comma system; quotation, dashes, ellipses, brackets, semicolons used as Danish editors use them.
12. **Orthography to the rules**: compounds, capitals, apostrophes, hyphens, loanword inflection (*jobbet, teamet, websites/websider*), the 2024 changes.
13. **Old orthography, receptive**: pre-1948 conventions; nineteenth-century inflection; Holberg-era vocabulary with a crib.
14. **Neighbouring languages, receptive**: Bokmål and Swedish read and heard for gist and detail; Nynorsk recognised.
15. **Varieties, receptive**: the main dialects, Copenhagen high and low, *multietnolekt*; the features that mark each.
16. **Word formation, productive**: coining acceptable compounds and derivations for new concepts (*klimaflygtning, skærmtid, afkriminalisere*); knowing when a coinage is a *særskrivning* error or an anglicism.
17. **Anglicisms**: which are accepted (*at chille, et update, at streame*), which are marked, and the Danish alternatives an editor would prefer.
18. **Numbers, dates, units and citations** in academic and legal prose.
19. **Discourse in speech**: turn-taking phrases, backchannels (*ja, mm, nemlig, præcis, okay*), repair, hedging, interruption management; the seminar register (*jeg vil mene, som jeg ser det, det er et godt spørgsmål, jeg er ikke sikker på, at ...*).
20. **Genres**: *analyserende, debatterende og reflekterende artikel*; *kronik*; *leder*; *anmeldelse*; *høringssvar*; *notat*; *referat*; *problemformulering*; their structures and stock phrases.
21. **Rhetoric**: *appelformer*, Toulmin, *argumentationskneb*, *retoriske spørgsmål*, *tretrinsraket*, *anafor*.
22. **Literary terms**: *fortæller, synsvinkel, komposition, fremstillingsform, in medias res, rammefortælling, indre monolog, dækket direkte tale, motiv, tema, perspektivering, intertekstualitet, periode*.
23. **Names of things**: Danish institutions, offices, laws and processes named correctly (*Folketingets Ombudsmand, Rigsrevisionen, Højesteret, forligskredsen, lovforslag L 123, betænkning, bekendtgørelse*).
24. **Pronunciation at C1**: stress, stød and rhythm native-like in reading aloud; sentence intonation; the reductions of fast speech produced, not only understood.
25. **Self-editing**: the ten-rule repair list maintained; every text edited once for structure and once for surface before it counts.

## Appendix E: C1 exit reading test

**Om at læse Blixen i dag**

Der findes forfattere, man vokser fra, og forfattere, man vokser ind i. Karen Blixen hører til de sidste, og det er ikke nogen selvfølge. Da jeg første gang forsøgte mig med *Vinter-Eventyr* som gymnasieelev, oplevede jeg sproget som en mur: de lange, snørklede perioder, de gammeldags ordformer, den insisterende brug af store bogstaver i en tid, hvor alle andre for længst havde lagt dem fra sig. Jeg gav op efter tredive sider og kaldte hende, med den sikkerhed man kun har som syttenårig, for utidssvarende. Det var først tyve år senere, da jeg af tilfældige grunde kom til at læse *Sorg-Agre* højt for en syg ven, at det gik op for mig, hvad jeg var gået glip af.

Det, der ved første møde virker som en manér, viser sig ved nærmere bekendtskab at være en metode. Blixen skriver ikke gammeldags, fordi hun ikke kan andet; hun skriver sådan, fordi hendes fortællinger handler om mennesker, der lever under en anden orden end vores, og fordi sproget skal bære den orden med sig. Den gamle godsejer i *Sorg-Agre*, som lader en bondekone høste en hel mark på én dag for at redde sin søn, er ikke en skurk, selvom en nutidig læser uvægerligt oplever ham sådan i begyndelsen. Han handler efter en lov, som han selv er underlagt, og fortællingens skandale er ikke hans hårdhed, men at han har ret på sine egne præmisser. Det er en tanke, der er svær at rumme for os, som er opdraget til at mene, at enhver orden, der koster menneskeliv, dermed har diskvalificeret sig selv. Blixen tvinger os til at holde tanken i luften lidt længere, end vi bryder os om.

Man kan indvende, at det er en aristokratisk fantasi, og indvendingen er ikke uden vægt. Blixen var baronesse, hun levede af en farm, der byggede på et kolonialt system, og hendes forestillinger om skæbne og storhed bærer tydelige spor af det. Men det er en fattig læsning, der standser der. Det interessante er ikke, at hun forsvarer en gammel verden, for det gør hun strengt taget ikke; det interessante er, at hun beskriver, hvad det koster at leve i en verden, hvor den gamle orden er brudt sammen, og ingen ny er trådt i stedet. Det er, når alt kommer til alt, også vores situation.

Dertil kommer sproget, som jeg altså engang tog for en mur. Læser man Blixen højt, hvad man bør, opdager man, at periodernes længde ikke er pynt, men rytme. Sætningerne er bygget til at blive sagt, og de har en musik, som moderne dansk prosa, med sine korte, kontante sætninger, sjældent tillader sig. Der er en grund til, at hendes tekster egner sig så godt til oplæsning, og til at Gabriel Axel kunne filme *Babettes Gæstebud* næsten replik for replik.

Det er ikke ensbetydende med, at alt hos Blixen er lige godt, eller at hun bør læses ukritisk. Nogle af fortællingerne er for lange, nogle af pointerne for demonstrative, og den kultur, der voksede op omkring hende på Rungstedlund i hendes sidste år, gjorde hende ikke nogen tjeneste. Men det er en forfatter, hvis bøger bliver bedre af, at man bliver ældre, og det kan ikke siges om ret mange. Måske skulle vi holde op med at give hende til syttenårige og i stedet give hende til fyrreårige, som har mistet noget og har brug for et sprog for tabet.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk, hele sætninger; spørgsmål 8–10 kræver fortolkning):

1. Hvad var skribentens første oplevelse af Blixens sprog, og hvor gammel var skribenten?
2. Hvilken anledning fik skribenten til at læse Blixen igen?
3. Hvad mener skribenten med, at det gammeldags sprog er "en metode" og ikke "en manér"?
4. Hvad består fortællingens skandale i, ifølge skribenten?
5. Hvilken indvending mod Blixen nævner skribenten, og hvordan svarer skribenten på den?
6. Hvad opdager man ifølge skribenten, når man læser Blixen højt?
7. Nævn to kritikpunkter, skribenten selv rejser mod Blixen.
8. Hvad er skribentens overordnede påstand, formuleret i én sætning?
9. Hvilken tone har teksten (fx ironisk, højtidelig, personlig, polemisk)? Begrund med to citater.
10. Hvad foreslår skribenten til sidst, og hvad ligger der i forslaget?

**Key:** 1. Sproget virkede som en mur (lange perioder, gammeldags former, store bogstaver); skribenten var sytten. 2. At læse *Sorg-Agre* højt for en syg ven, tyve år senere. 3. At sproget bevidst bærer en anden orden, som personerne lever under; det er et valg, ikke en begrænsning. 4. At godsejeren har ret på sine egne præmisser; ikke at han er hård. 5. At det er en aristokratisk fantasi (baronesse, kolonial farm); skribenten svarer, at Blixen ikke forsvarer den gamle orden, men beskriver, hvad det koster at leve efter dens sammenbrud, hvilket også er vores situation. 6. At de lange sætninger er rytme og musik, bygget til at blive sagt. 7. Two of: nogle fortællinger er for lange; nogle pointer for demonstrative; kulturen omkring hende på Rungstedlund. 8. Blixen er en forfatter, man vokser ind i, fordi hendes stil og hendes emne først åbner sig for en læser, der selv har erfaret tab. 9. Personlig og essayistisk med lette ironiske træk (accept any well-grounded answer), e.g. "med den sikkerhed man kun har som syttenårig" and "Det er, når alt kommer til alt, også vores situation." 10. At give Blixen til fyrreårige i stedet for til syttenårige; forslaget rummer påstanden om, at hendes bøger kræver livserfaring, især erfaringen af tab.

- **Television** (Danish services at the time of writing; catalogues change): *Herrens veje* seasons 1–2 on Netflix Denmark; *Borgen* seasons 1–3 and *Riget, Magten og Æren* on Netflix Denmark; *Riget* I–II (restored) and *Riget Exodus* on Viaplay; *1864* on Viaplay; *Deadline* (DR1, Monday to Thursday 17:30, repeated on DR2, about 30 minutes, on DRTV), *Debatten*, *Casper og Mandrilaftalen*, *Drengene fra Angora* and *Rytteriet* on DRTV; *Tæt på sandheden* ended in 2025 and its archive stays on DRTV, where DR's new satire programme *Kommissionen* also runs; *Arvingerne* has left DRTV, so use it wherever it is offered or substitute *Bedrag* season 1 (DRTV); *Broen* is rental only (SF Anytime, Blockbuster.dk).
- **Films**: *Den eneste ene* (Nordisk Film+, Netflix, Viaplay), *Kongekabale* (Nordisk Film+), *Ordet* and *Vredens dag* (Filmstriben, free with a library card, under "Fokus på Dreyer"), *I Kina spiser de hunde* (Netflix, Nordisk Film+), *Pusher* (Netflix), *En kongelig affære* (Netflix, Viaplay, Nordisk Film+), *Under sandet* (Netflix, Nordisk Film+), *Idioterne* (Nordisk Film+), *Dronningen* (Netflix, Viaplay), *Submarino* (Viaplay), *Drabet* (Nordisk Film+). Rent anything missing on Blockbuster.dk, SF Anytime or Apple TV.
- **Press**: Weekendavisen (Digital Plus about 199 kr a month, with audio versions of the articles and the podcast *Avistid*), Information (about 244 kr a month; half price under 30) or Politiken (daily), Zetland (audio articles), videnskab.dk (free science journalism), tidsskrift.dk (free Danish academic journals). Information's kronik is 7400 characters and is submitted through the form at information.dk; Politiken's kronik is about 2000 words.
- **Podcasts**: on DR Lyd *Genstart*, *P1 Orientering*, *Supertanker*, *K-Live*, *Magten* (DR's politics podcast, which replaced *Slotsholmen*) and *Skønlitteratur på P1* (DR's literature programme); *Bogfolk* (Politiken's weekly literature podcast); *Altinget Ajour* (politics, 15 minutes, Monday to Thursday) and *Borgen Unplugged*; *Fuld af viden* (Folkeuniversitetet's weekly lecture podcast) and the Videnslyd catalogue; the *Tænkepauser* conversations.
- **Neighbouring languages**: nrk.no and svt.se for news text and radio; NRK's television is largely geoblocked outside Norway and SVT Play shows only SVT's own productions abroad, so use text, radio and *Broen*; Sveriges Radio P1 for Swedish audio; *Norden i Skolen* (nordeniskolen.org) for free *nabosprog* teaching material; dialekt.ku.dk for Danish dialects.
- **Studieprøven**: danskogproever.dk ("Om Studieprøven") describes the test (reading 90 minutes in two sittings: 15 short-answer questions on a text collection, then a gapped text, multiple choice and a vocabulary test; writing 180 minutes, one of three society-related tasks, at least 400 words; oral about 30 minutes with three prepared topics, one drawn, a 5-minute presentation and a 15-minute interview; no listening paper; pass mark 02 in each part) and publishes the example papers with keys and the written assessment guide. The oral assessment material is on SIRI's examiner-training site behind a login.
- **AI instructions** are in C1-005. For the neighbouring languages the assistant can be asked to speak Norwegian or Swedish in a voice session; use it for ten minutes a week in Weeks 21–29.
