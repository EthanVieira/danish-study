# Level A1: Breakthrough

**Goal at exit:** you can introduce yourself, describe your family, home, job and daily routine, handle shopping, directions and simple small talk, understand slow clear Danish on familiar topics, read simple texts, and write short simple messages. You know roughly 800 to 1000 words, the core of Danish grammar, and you can pronounce Danish well enough that a Dane would understand you.

**Time:** about 200 hours over 14 weeks: about 159 h of timed steps (287 steps in all), about 10 h of checks, and about 33 h of the two dailies (Anki, 15 minutes a day from the first day; the dagbog, 10 minutes a day from Week 8). Weeks 1 and 2 are lighter (about 9 h and 5 h of steps); from Week 3 a guide week holds between 9.5 and 15 h of steps. That is about 2 hours a day, 7 days a week. If your days are shorter, keep the order and let a guide week take longer than seven days: at 90 minutes a day the level takes about 22 weeks.

**Prerequisite:** read [README.md](README.md) fully, especially the six standard methods. The step text below refers to them by name (LRS cycle, Anki rules, Recording protocol, Correction protocol, Intensive/Extensive, scheduled input).

**How a day works at A1:** first the dailies: clear Anki (from A1-0004, the first day), then write the dagbog entry (from A1-0139, Week 8). Then do the next unticked step, and the one after it if you have time. Nothing else runs alongside. Every Pimsleur lesson, song, series episode, picture book and listening session is a numbered step, placed where it belongs in the order (Method 6). The "Series: …" steps start at A1-0027 (Week 2) with *Bamses Billedbog* and switch to *Gurli Gris* in Block 3. The "Reading: …" steps start at A1-0087 (Week 5), when *Halfdans ABC* is finished: one picture book a week from the free-reading shelf, plus the two easy readers. The "Listening: …" steps start at A1-0148 (Week 8), when Pimsleur ends. At A1 all of this input is extensive and Casual: no cards, no summaries unless a step asks for one.

## Patterns

A step that names one of these patterns is done exactly as the pattern says, and it is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done.

- **CD unit pattern.** Each Complete Danish unit is four steps:
  - **(a) Dialogue**: LRS cycle on every dialogue in the unit. Done when all six LRS stages are done for every dialogue and the recording is saved.
  - **(b) Grammar and exercises**: read the grammar notes; copy each rule into the notebook with the book's example plus one example of your own; do every exercise in the unit in writing; check against the key; redo every wrong item and write in the notebook *why* it was wrong. Done when all exercises are done and checked, and the wrong ones redone with reasons written.
  - **(c) Vocabulary and shadowing**: every new word in the unit's vocabulary list goes into Anki per the Anki rules (usually 25–40 cards; add them all now, and Anki's limit of 15 new cards a day spreads them over the next days). Then "shadow live" all dialogues 3 more times and record the last dialogue of the unit. Done when all unit vocabulary is in Anki, 3 live shadows are done and one is recorded.
  - **(d) Production**: write 8 sentences about your own life using this unit's grammar and words. Correction protocol. Copy the corrected sentences by hand into the notebook. Done when the corrected sentences are in the notebook. From Unit 6, (d) is part of the week's dagbog correction step instead (see Block 3).
- **Pimsleur pattern.** Sit or walk somewhere you can speak aloud. Answer every prompt aloud *before* the native speaker does. Never skip forward. Done when the lesson has ended and you answered at least 80 percent of the prompts in time. If you answered fewer, leave the step unticked and do the same lesson again at your next session.
- **DHON chapter pattern.** Each *Dansk her og nu* chapter is two steps.
  - **Part 1**: *Tekst og dialog*: play each dialogue 3 times blind, then read along. *Grammatik og øvelser*: read the notes, do every exercise, redo any exercise scoring under 80 percent. Done when every exercise shows 80 percent or above.
  - **Part 2**: *Lyt og forstå*: do every listening exercise, replaying at most twice. *Danmark og danskerne*: read it once (it is about Danish life; it may be above your level, just skim). *Ordliste*: add 15 new words from the chapter to Anki (skip any already there). Finally, play the chapter's episode of the DHON podcast blind, twice, sitting down, and write a 2-line English gist. Done when every listening exercise shows 80 percent or above, 15 cards are added, and the podcast episode has been played twice and the gist written.
- **Song pattern.** Find the Danish lyrics (search the title plus "tekst") and a recording on YouTube. Listen twice following the lyrics. Sing along 10 times, the last 3 without looking at the lyrics. Then sing it once alone, without the recording. Done when you have sung it through alone without the lyrics.
- **Song recall pattern.** Without listening first, sing the song from memory. Write the lyrics from memory and check them against the text. Sing along with the recording 3 times, then once more alone. Done when you can sing it from memory with no lyric errors.
- **Series pattern.** Episodes are counted in the order your service lists them, starting from the top of the list: "episode 5" is the fifth on the list. Watch the named episodes in order, with no subtitles. When the title says "with rewatch", watch the last episode of the sitting again with Danish subtitles if the service has them (without, if it has none) and write down what the step asks for from that episode. Log one tracker line per episode. No cards. Done when the episodes are logged. If you cannot get *Gurli Gris*, stay with *Bamses Billedbog* in Blocks 3–5: each week's first Series step is then the next *Bamses Billedbog* episode and its second Series step the next two (in Week 13, three and three).
- **Picture-book pattern.** Each picture book from the free-reading shelf gets up to three sittings. A sitting lasts the stated time: when you reach the last page before the time is up, go back to page 1.
  - **First read**: read the book aloud, slowly, guessing the meaning from the pictures.
  - **Second read**: if eReolen Go has the audiobook, listen to it once while following the pages; then (or otherwise) read the book aloud again.
  - **Third read**: read it silently, straight through.
  - In every sitting, look up at most 3–5 words and make no cards. Log one tracker line (title, pages). After the last sitting of a book, add it to `canon-tracker.md`. If you cannot get the named title, take another book of the same series or, failing that, any other Danish picture book with short text on each page.
- **Listening pattern.** 50 minutes with no pausing and no notes; this is the one kind of step you can do on a walk, on a commute or over the dishes. Play the named items in the order given. If the list ends before the 50 minutes are up, start it again from the first item. Done when the 50 minutes are up. Everything on a list is audio you have already studied, so never swap in something new.
- **Dagbog correction pattern.** Run all the dagbog entries since the last correction step through the Correction protocol in one go and copy the corrections into the notebook. Done when the corrections are copied.

## Block 1: Sounds, alphabet, numbers (Weeks 1–2)

Danish sound is the hardest thing you will learn. This block goes before any grammar on purpose. Do not rush it.

### Week 1

About 4.5 h of steps, after the setup block.

- [ ] **A1-0015** [Study · Deep · 45 min] The alphabet.
      Do: Danish has 29 letters: a–z plus æ, ø, å (in that order, at the end). On SPK, open the alphabet page and listen to each letter name 3 times, repeating aloud. Search YouTube for "alfabetsangen" (a Danish alphabet song for children); sing along 5 times. Write the alphabet by hand 5 times, saying each letter name aloud. Then spell aloud, letter by letter: København, Ærø, Åbenrå, Skagen, Odense, Esbjerg.
      Done when: you can say all 29 letter names in order from memory in under 30 seconds, and can spell your own name and the six place names aloud with Danish letter names.

- [ ] **A1-0016** [Study · Deep · 60 min] Vowels.
      Do: on SPK, work through the vowel section of the pronunciation guide (if any page you need is behind the paywall, either pay the one-off fee for this block or use Danish Mastery's and Mic's Languages' vowel videos plus the English Wikipedia article "Danish phonology" instead). Danish has 9 vowel letters (a e i o u y æ ø å) but around 20 vowel sounds, because each letter has a long and short version and several letters have more than one quality. For each letter, listen to the example words 5 times each and repeat. In the notebook, make a table: letter, long example word, short example word (use SPK's examples). Look up each example word on DDO and play its audio to confirm.
      Done when: the table is complete with at least one long and one short example word for every vowel letter, and you have said every example word at least 5 times aloud.

- [ ] **A1-0017** [Study · Deep · 45 min] Consonants that behave differently from English.
      Do: on SPK, study the consonant section, concentrating on:
      1. Soft d (the "blødt d"): after a vowel, d becomes a sound like a lazy English "th" in "the" made with the tongue low: *mad, gade, hedder, bad, side*.
      2. Soft or silent g after a vowel: *dag, bog, meget, jeg* (in *jeg* it is a "y" sound).
      3. R: made in the throat (like French), and after a vowel it becomes a vowel-like "ah/uh": *mor, bær, er, her*.
      4. V after a vowel often becomes a "w"-like sound: *havn, hav, navn*. (In *havde* the v is silent.)
      5. Silent h before v and j: *hvad, hvor, hvem, hjem, hjælp*.
      6. Endings: *-et* and *-ede* are soft: *huset, boede*. The suffix *-ig* is pronounced "-i": *rigtig, dejlig*.
      Write these 6 rules in the notebook with all 25 example words. Look up every word on DDO and play it. Say each 5 times.
      Done when: the 6 rules and 25 words are in the notebook and you have heard and said each word.

- [ ] **A1-0018** [Study · Deep · 45 min] Stød.
      Do: read SPK's explanation of stød (a brief glottal catch in the middle of a syllable, like the "catch" in a Cockney "bu'er" but much shorter). Then on DDO, look up and play these minimal pairs several times each, listening only for the catch: *hun / hund*, *man / mand*, *mor / mord*, *bønner / bønder*, *anden* (the duck) / *anden* (the other, second), *tanken* (the thought) / *tanken* (the tank). In the first four pairs the second word has stød (*hund, mand, mord, bønder*). In *anden* it is the first: the duck has stød, "the other, second" has none. For *tanken*, DDO shows separate entries; check which has it. Write the pairs in the notebook and mark which has stød. Then do a self-test: have a random-number generator pick 10 words from the list, play each on DDO without looking at which entry you clicked (cover the screen), and decide "stød or not".
      Done when: you score 8 out of 10 on the self-test. If not, repeat the listening and retest; do this until you pass.

- [ ] **A1-0019** [Study · Focused · 40 min] Pronunciation videos.
      Do: on YouTube, watch three videos, one each about the soft d, the stød, and Danish vowels: search "Mic's Languages" plus "soft d", "stød", "vowels" and take the first result for each; if the channel has none on a topic, take the first Danish Mastery result instead. Write 5 practical tips you did not get from SPK into the notebook.
      Done when: 5 tips are written down.

- [ ] **A1-0020** [Drill · Focused · 40 min] First 30 Anki cards: greetings and survival phrases.
      Do: create these 30 cards following the Anki rules (Danish phrase on the front, English on the back, audio from DDO or Forvo where possible): *hej · hej hej · farvel · godmorgen · goddag · godaften · godnat · tak · mange tak · tusind tak · selv tak · undskyld · ja · nej · jo (yes, contradicting a negative) · måske · værsgo · velbekomme · Hvordan går det? · Det går godt. · Jeg hedder ... · Hvad hedder du? · Jeg kommer fra ... · Jeg bor i ... · Jeg forstår ikke. · Kan du sige det igen? · Taler du engelsk? · Jeg taler kun lidt dansk. · Hyggeligt at møde dig. · Vi ses.*
      Done when: 30 cards exist and you have done today's review.

### Week 2

About 5 h of steps.

      Done when: lesson 1 finished with the 80 percent rule met.

- [ ] **A1-0023** [Input · Deep · 20 min] *Halfdans ABC*, letters A to C.
      Do: get the book (Appendix I). Each page is a short rhyme for one letter, with Ib Spang Olsen's illustrations. Read each page aloud, slowly, once, guessing meaning from the pictures first. Then look up every word on DDO, play its audio, and read the page aloud again 3 times trying to keep the beat of the verse. Add 2 words per letter to Anki (6 cards).
      Done when: pages A–C read aloud at least 4 times each and 6 cards added.

- [ ] **A1-0024** [Study · Deep · 60 min] Numbers 21–1000 and the vigesimal system.

Units come *before* tens joined by *og*: 21 = *enogtyve*, 55 = *femoghalvtreds*, 99 = *nioghalvfems*, 234 = *to hundrede (og) fireogtredive*. Write out 20 numbers in words in the notebook. Add the 10 tens to Anki. Watch one YouTube video on Danish numbers (search "Mic's Languages Danish numbers" and take the first result; if there is none, the first DanishClass101 result). Then: random generator 21–999, say 20 numbers aloud.
      Done when: 18 of 20 random numbers said correctly within 3 seconds, and you can recite the tens 20–100 from memory.

- [ ] **A1-0025** [Study · Focused · 30 min] PIM lesson 2 (Pimsleur pattern).

- [ ] **A1-0026** [Input · Deep · 20 min] *Halfdans ABC*, letters D to G.

      Done when: pages D–G read aloud at least 4 times each and 8 cards added.

- [ ] **A1-0027** [Input · Casual · 20 min] Series: *Bamses Billedbog*, episode 1.

From now on a series is always running (Method 6), as numbered "Series: …" steps: *Bamses Billedbog* through Block 2, *Gurli Gris* from Block 3.
      Done when: 5 recognised items written in the notebook and the episode logged.

- [ ] **A1-0028** [Study · Focused · 30 min] PIM lesson 3 (Pimsleur pattern).

- [ ] **A1-0029** [Input · Deep · 20 min] *Halfdans ABC*, letters H to J.
      Do: same procedure as A1-0023. 6 cards.
      Done when: pages H–J read aloud at least 4 times each and 6 cards added.

- [ ] **A1-0030** [Produce · Focused · 15 min] Baseline recording.
      Do: Recording protocol. Read aloud: the alphabet, the numbers 0–20, the tens, and the last 10 phrases of A1-0020 (from *Jeg hedder ...* to *Vi ses.*). Keep this file; you will listen to it again at the A1 exit.
      Done when: `YYYY-MM-DD-A1-0030.m4a` is in `recordings`.

- [ ] **A1-0031** [Check] Sound check (end of Week 2).

      4. Random numbers 21–999: 18/20 (A1-0024), retaken today.

      6. Every step so far is ticked (PIM 1–3 included).

## Block 2: Core course, part 1 (Weeks 3–7)

From here on, each Complete Danish unit is done as four steps (a, b, c, d) by the CD unit pattern, and each Dansk her og nu chapter as two steps by the DHON chapter pattern (both in the Patterns section at the top of this file). A Pimsleur lesson follows on most days until lesson 30 (Week 8). ## Appendix A: How Danish works (primer)

1. **Family.** Danish is North Germanic, a sibling of Norwegian and Swedish and a cousin of English. Much basic vocabulary is recognisable: *hus, bog, komme, drikke, over, under, hånd, fod*.
2. **Spelling is old, pronunciation is new.** Danish spelling reflects how the language sounded centuries ago. Modern speech has reduced or dropped many consonants and shifted vowels. You must learn every word by ear as well as by eye. Never assume a spelling tells you the sound.
3. **Two genders.** Common gender (*en bil*) and neuter (*et hus*). About 75 percent of nouns are *en*-words. Learn the article with every noun; there is no reliable rule.
4. **The definite article is a suffix.** *bil → bilen* (the car), *hus → huset* (the house), *biler → bilerne*, *huse → husene*. A separate *den/det/de* appears only when an adjective is present: *den røde bil*.
5. **Verbs do not change for person.** *jeg er, du er, han er, vi er*. Present tense is the infinitive plus *-r*. This makes Danish verbs far easier than French or German.
6. **Verb second.** In a main clause the finite verb is always the second element. Put anything else first and the subject moves behind the verb: *I dag spiser jeg fisk.*
7. **Ikke moves.** *ikke* (not) comes after the verb in main clauses and before the verb in subordinate clauses. This is the most common grammar error at every level up to B2.
8. **Stød.** A glottal catch inside a syllable that distinguishes words (*hun* she / *hund* dog). It does not exist in the southern dialects but it is standard in Copenhagen Danish, which is what all your resources teach.
9. **Vowels.** Nine letters, around twenty sounds, long and short. *a* alone has three or four qualities. Vowel precision matters more than consonant precision for being understood.
10. **Reduction.** Spoken Danish drops and blurs: *meget* sounds like "mah-eth", *det* like "de", *jeg* like "yai", *ikke* like "egg-eh". Learner materials are slower and clearer than real Danes. The children's TV and films in this guide are there to bridge the gap.
11. **Numbers are vigesimal from 50 up.** *tres* = 3 × 20, *firs* = 4 × 20, *halvtreds* = 2.5 × 20, *halvfjerds* = 3.5 × 20, *halvfems* = 4.5 × 20. Units precede tens: *fireoghalvfjerds* = 74.
12. **Politeness works differently.** Everyone is *du*; the formal *De* is essentially gone. There is no word for "please": use *gerne* ("Jeg vil gerne have ..."), *tak* at the end of requests, and *Må jeg bede om ...*. *Tak* is used far more than English "thanks" (*tak for mad, tak for i dag, tak for sidst*).

## Appendix B: A1 grammar checklist

By the end of A1 you must be able to produce all of the following correctly and without hesitation.

1. **Noun gender and articles**: *en bil / et hus*; definite *bilen / huset*.
2. **Plurals**: *-er* (*biler*), *-e* (*huse*), no ending (*sko, år*), and the common irregulars *mand/mænd, barn/børn, bog/bøger, by/byer, fod/fødder, tand/tænder, hånd/hænder, mor/mødre, far/fædre, bror/brødre*. Definite plural adds *-ne*: *bilerne, husene, børnene*.
3. **Adjective agreement**: *en stor bil, et stort hus, store biler, den store bil, min store bil*; *lille/små*; *ny/nyt/nye*.
4. **Personal pronouns**: *jeg/mig, du/dig, han/ham, hun/hende, den, det, vi/os, I/jer, de/dem*.
5. **Possessives**: *min/mit/mine, din/dit/dine, hans, hendes, dens/dets, vores, jeres, deres*; *sin/sit/sine* when the owner is the third-person subject.
6. **Present tense**: infinitive + *r*; *er, har, ved, gør, kan, vil, skal, må, bør*.
7. **Past tense**: *-ede* (*boede*), *-te* (*spiste*), and the irregulars *var, havde, gik, kom, så, fik, tog, gav, sagde, drak, sov, blev, vidste, gjorde*.
8. **Perfect**: *har* + participle (*har boet, har spist*); *er* with movement/change (*er kommet, er gået, er blevet*).
9. **Modals** with bare infinitive: *Jeg kan svømme. Jeg skal arbejde. Jeg vil gerne have ... Du må ikke ...*
10. **Main-clause word order**: verb second; inversion after a fronted element; *ikke* after the verb; yes/no questions by inversion; question words *hvad, hvem, hvor, hvornår, hvorfor, hvordan, hvilken/hvilket/hvilke, hvor mange, hvor meget*.
11. **Existential *der***: *Der er en kat i haven. Der ligger en bog på bordet.*
12. **Core prepositions**: *i, på, til, fra, hos, med, om, ved, over, under, foran, bag, ved siden af*.
13. **Time**: clock including *halv* (next hour), *kvart over/i*; days; months; dates with ordinals; *i dag, i går, i morgen, i aften, om morgenen, om mandagen, i weekenden*.
14. **Numbers** 0–1000 and ordinals 1st–31st.
15. **Connectors**: *og, men, eller, så, at, fordi, når, hvis* (recognise all; use *og, men, eller, at, fordi* actively) with subordinate-clause *ikke* before the verb.
16. **Common adverbs and quantifiers**: *meget, lidt, også, ikke, aldrig, altid, tit/ofte, gerne, for* (too), *nogen/noget/nogle, ingen/intet, mange, meget*.
17. **Imperative**: the verb stem alone: *Kom! Spis! Vent! Sig det igen!*

## Appendix I: Resource acquisition notes

- **Complete Danish Beginner to Intermediate Course (Teach Yourself)**, Bente Elsworth, 2017 edition, ISBN 9781444194982, 18 units plus a verb reference, glossary and revision section. Audio is free via the Teach Yourself Library app or library.teachyourself.com. Amazon, Saxo.com, or the publisher.
- **Pimsleur Danish Level 1.** Pimsleur app subscription (all languages) or Audible, where it is sold in five-lesson packs (1–5, 6–10, ... 26–30). Only Level 1 exists for Danish. Reading lessons start after lesson 11; do them.
- **Dansk her og nu**: danskherognu.dk. Free, made with support from the Danish Integration Ministry. 12 chapters with text, grammar, listening, culture and word list; companion podcast at podcast.danskherognu.dk. Register with an email.
- **Anki**: apps.ankiweb.net. Do not use a pre-made shared deck as your main deck; making your own cards is part of the learning. The shared deck "Danish 1000 Most Common Words With Pronunciation" (id 981694721) is used once, in Week 12 (from A1-0224), as a coverage check and then deleted.
- **Den Danske Ordbog**: ordnet.dk/ddo. Free. Every headword has audio and full inflection.
- **speakdanish.dk**: a long-running site with a paid dialogue course and a pronunciation guide (letters, stress, difficult words) with slow and normal-speed audio for every word. Use the pronunciation guide; the dialogue course is optional.
- **Danish: An Essential Grammar**, Tom Lundskær-Nielsen and Philip Holmes, Routledge, 2nd edition 2011, ISBN 9780415496896. Chapters are by topic (nouns, adjectives, numerals, pronouns, verbs, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, word order). This is the reference for the whole programme; its big sibling *Danish: A Comprehensive Grammar* (2nd ed. 2010) is added at B2.
- **YouTube channels**: "Mic's Languages" (Michael S. Rasmussen, a native Danish teacher, strong on stress and pronunciation), "Danish Mastery" (a native Dane; short phrase and grammar videos, multi-hour grammar deep dives, and H.C. Andersen stories read and explained, which you will use at A2–B1), "DanishClass101" (Innovative Language; hundreds of short lessons).
- **Halfdans ABC**, Halfdan Rasmussen, illustrated by Ib Spang Olsen (1967, now Lindhardt og Ringhof). In print; buy from Saxo.com, Plusbog.dk, or any Danish bookshop; in every Danish library. The edition "Halfdans ABC med musik-CD" (ISBN 9788711374740) has the rhymes set to music by Danish artists; the album is also on Spotify.
- **De små synger** (Gyldendal / Høst & Søn, 134 songs, essentially unchanged since 1948, ISBN 9788714195519). The standard Danish children's songbook. In print. Audio: "De små synger CD" (Gyldendal), or search each song title on YouTube or Spotify.
- **DRTV**: dr.dk/drtv, free with a profile. Children's content is under Ramasjang. At the time of writing *Bamses Billedbog*, *Kaj og Andrea*, *Matador*, *Borgen* and *Forbrydelsen* were all there. If you live in Denmark, DRTV works directly (and, while you are temporarily abroad in the EU, via MitID login). Otherwise it is geoblocked. Fallbacks for the Series steps: the same series on another service (check JustWatch, Denmark), official clips on YouTube, or DVDs. DR's Danish subtitles on older children's programmes are inconsistent; where an episode has none, use the fallback in the step.
- **Library services** (A1-0009): a Danish public library card gives free access to **eReolen Go** (ereolengo.dk: children's e-books and audiobooks, including many picture books and easy readers), **eReolen** (ereolen.dk: adult e-books and audiobooks) and **Filmstriben** (filmstriben.dk: Danish films, including children's films). You sign up with MitID on your municipality library's website. Loan limits are set by each municipality. Paid fallbacks: Mofibo, BookBeat or Nextory for books.
