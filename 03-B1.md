# Level B1: Threshold

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A2 reached (step A2-0613 ticked). The README methods still apply. The recurring activities you know from A2 (the podcast pattern, dictation, AI voice sessions, monologues, the dagbog correction) continue as numbered steps, with the changes described under Patterns below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow the main points of clear standard Danish on familiar topics (news, a TV drama, a documentary, a conversation about work or opinions), handle almost any everyday situation including bureaucracy and the doctor, tell a story or explain an opinion in connected speech with reasons, read a children's novel, a young-adult novel, official information and newspaper articles, and write about 200 words of connected text in the right register (a formal letter to the municipality, an opinion piece, a personal narrative). Vocabulary about 4000 words. You know the complete grammar of Danish, including the full word-order system, both passives, phrasal verbs with their stress, indirect speech, conditionals, and the connector system, and your pronunciation has correct unit stress and timing. This is the level of Prøve i Dansk 2 and of the citizenship-test reading material.

**Time:** about 740 hours over 35 guide weeks. The 1093 numbered steps take about 640 hours, which is about 18½ hours in a typical guide week (less in Weeks 1–9, and about 9 in each of the two exit weeks). The two dailies take about 95 hours more (Anki about 70, the dagbog about 25). The milestone and exit checks carry no time tags; allow about 25 hours for them on top. At 3½ hours a day, 6 days a week, a guide week takes about a calendar week. About 110 hours of the steps are Casual listening steps (Genstart, replays, audiobook re-listens, documentaries, Fluent Fiction) that you can do on a commute or over chores, about 110 hours are series and films, and about 70 hours are book sittings; the rest is desk work, news, articles and speaking. If you have 2 hours a day, 6 days a week, keep the order and let the level take about 64 calendar weeks.

**What changes at B1** (Appendix A): the culture track is now in Danish. The textbook is Danish-made. *Matador* is the television spine, one episode in each of 24 weeks. You read your first unabridged novels and Andersen in the original, and the official citizenship-test material becomes your non-fiction reader. Grammar is a systematic pass through *Danish: An Essential Grammar* from the first chapter to the last. News moves from easy-Danish articles to DR's normal news and, from Week 9, TV Avisen. Reading, series and listening (README Method 6) are in every week as numbered steps: whenever no studied book is open, the next book on the free-reading shelf is (Kirkegaard, Kaaberbøl, Teller); *Matador* runs to Week 25 and *Krøniken* after it, with a lighter casual series alongside; and the listening steps add audiobook re-listens, a DR Lyd documentary rotation and, from Week 21, *Fluent Fiction*.

**How a day works at B1:** do the two dailies, then the next unticked step. The dailies are Anki (all due cards, then 20 new cards) and the dagbog (8–10 sentences, six days a week). Everything else in this file is a numbered step that you do once, in one sitting, in the order given: textbook chapters, grammar, book sittings, series episodes, listening, news, AI voice sessions, monologues, dictations, articles and songs. Reading, series and listening are scheduled as steps like everything else; nothing is left for spare moments, and you never decide what comes next or how often. If you have time left after a step, do the one after it. Inside each week the steps are ordered so that desk work alternates with reading, watching and listening, and the week ends with its check.

## Patterns

Every step in this file is one sitting. Each recurring kind of step is defined once here. A step that names a pattern is done exactly as the pattern says and is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done. The step gives the specifics (which episode, which pages, which topic); the pattern gives the method. Where a step or a pattern says "the newest" or "the next in order", that rule decides the content. You do not choose.

**The dailies at B1.** Anki: all due cards, then 20 new cards a day; at least a third of new cards are sentence cards (B1-0006). Dagbog: 8–10 sentences on six days a week. Do both before the day's first step. They are the only things that recur outside the numbered steps.

**Listening and news**

- **Podcast pattern** (DIØ to Week 33, SDP to Week 20): take the next episode in order after the last one you studied. Listen blind twice. Write a 5-sentence summary. Read the transcript, looking up what you need, and score your summary out of 5 against it. Listen once more with the text. Shadow one 60-second segment (the first full minute after the introduction). Add 8 cards. From Week 10 the pattern is lighter: shadow 30 seconds and add no cards.
- **News pattern**: a news sitting has two parts, in this order.
  1. *The broadcast.* Weeks 1–8: the newest *Ultra Nyt* on DRTV, watched as at A2; write its three headlines in Danish. From Week 9: the most recent 21:00 edition of *TV Avisen* on DRTV (the 18:30 edition if the 21:00 is not up yet), on demand, no pausing. To Week 20, turn on the player's Danish subtitles when the on-demand edition offers them; otherwise watch without. From Week 21, always without. Afterwards write the five headlines in Danish and check them against dr.dk/nyheder.
  2. *The article.* Weeks 1–4: the newest article on dr.dk/ligetil that you have not read. From Week 5: the newest article under "Seneste nyt" on dr.dk/nyheder (or in the DR Nyheder app) that you have not read; skip any that runs over about 600 words. Read it with at most 8 lookups and write a two-sentence Danish summary in the notebook.

  A step titled "Article" or "Articles" uses part 2 alone; "broadcast only" uses part 1 alone.
- **Replay pattern** (Weeks 1–9): play last week's DIØ episode and then last week's SDP episode straight through, with no text and no output (in Week 1, the last two episodes you studied at A2). If both end before 30 minutes are up, play the DIØ episode again.
- **Genstart pattern** (from Week 5): the newest episode of DR's daily news podcast that you have not heard (25–35 minutes), extensive: no pausing, no dictionary. Afterwards write the topic and three things you understood, in Danish, then read dr.dk's description of the episode to check. Fallback for Weeks 5–8 only: if you could not write three things after a Genstart step, the next Genstart step in that week is two editions of DR's *Tiden* instead (three stories in 15 minutes; DR has announced that *Tiden* closes on 1 January 2027, after which use three of *Radioavisen*'s bulletins).
- **Re-listen pattern** (from Week 10): the audiobook of a book you have already read, with no text and no output. Divide the audiobook's running time by the number of parts in the step title; the step is that part. The part counts are set so that a part runs about half an hour. The order is fixed: *Otto er et næsehorn*, *Orla Frøsnapper*, *Lille Virgil*, *Hodja fra Pjort*, *Busters verden*, *Vildheks: Ildprøven*, *Skammerens datter*, then Kirkegaard's *Albert* (the one title here you have not read; check eReolen Go, and if it is not there use *Frode og alle de andre rødder*), then *Barndom*. If you cannot get an audiobook, play the previous title on the list again in its place. If you switched from *Barndom* to *Slangens gave* in B1-0891, the six *Barndom* parts are the first three hours of the *Slangens gave* audiobook, in half-hour parts.
- **Documentary pattern** (from Week 12): one episode of 30–45 minutes of the programme named in the step, from the DR Lyd archive, extensive. Which episode: for *Kampen om historien* (DR P1's history debate), the episode whose subject is closest to the IND pages you read this week, or the newest one if none fits; for *P1 Dokumentar* (narrated documentaries) and *Videnskabens verden* (science), the newest episode you have not heard. If a programme's archive is no longer on DR Lyd, take the newest *P1 Dokumentar* episode instead (if that is the missing one, *Videnskabens verden*). No output beyond one tracker line: programme and title.
- **Fluent Fiction pattern** (from Week 21): the two newest stories at fluentfiction.com/da that you have not heard, one after the other. Listen once to each; read the transcript only if you were lost. No cards, no output.

All "Listening:" steps are Casual: you can do them on a commute, on a walk or over chores. They are still done in order, like every other step.

**Speaking**

- **Voice-session pattern**: 15 minutes of spoken Danish with the AI voice mode, using the instruction saved in B1-0006. Open with the topic in the step title and stay on it; let the assistant ask follow-up questions. Speak in full sentences; if you cannot find a word, describe it in Danish. Afterwards, in text, ask for the list of errors and copy any recurring one into the notebook. For a roleplay topic, tell the assistant which role it plays (Appendix F part 4) before you start.
- **Monologue pattern**: Recording protocol (README Method 3), 3–4 minutes on the topic in the step title (Appendix F part 2), then transcribed and run through the Correction protocol. From Week 26 the topics come round again: after recording, listen to your first recording of the same topic and note one improvement.
- **Dictation pattern**: two minutes of the episode named in the step, starting three minutes in. Play it in 5-second chunks and write every word. Compare with the transcript, count the wrong or missing words and log the percentage in `writing/dictations.md`. Rewrite each wrong word three times.

**Writing and reading**

- **Bank-text pattern**: the Appendix I task in the step title, about 200 words unless the task says otherwise, closed book. Correction protocol. Clean copy with its error count in `writing/portfolio-B1.md`. When the title says "again", write the task fresh without looking at your first version, then compare the two and note the change in the error count.
- **Feature pattern** (the even weeks 18–30, where it takes the bank text's place): the newest article of 1,500–2,000 words on dr.dk marked *Baggrund* or *Explainer* that you have not read. In Week 26 it is the article page of the newest *P1 Dokumentar* episode; if that page is under 1,500 words, take a dr.dk feature as in the other weeks. Read it extensively with at most 5 lookups and write a 3-sentence Danish summary.
- **Samvirke pattern** (from Week 12): the newest article on samvirke.dk that you have not read (food, consumer topics, everyday life; plain Danish), extensive, at most 5 lookups. Cards: 3 from the week's first Samvirke article, 2 from its second.
- **Wikipedia pattern** (Weeks 20–32): the Danish Wikipedia article named in the step, read extensively from the top for about 20 minutes or to the end, whichever comes first. 5 cards.
- **Dagbog correction**: run the week's six entries through the Correction protocol in one batch and copy each corrected sentence into the notebook.
- **Song pattern**: the Appendix J song in the step title, with the lyrics from hojskolesangbogen.dk, Genius, or a search for the title plus "tekst". Read all the verses with every word looked up, then learn the chorus and one verse by heart, singing along with the recording. When a step names two songs, do one verse of each.
- **IND pattern** (Weeks 8–28): the official learning material for Indfødsretsprøven, read in order in 63 sittings. The step gives the pages (for the 246-page edition; if yours has a different page count, divide it into 63 equal parts and take the part with the step's number). First play the official MP3 for these pages (the chapter's file, from where the last sitting stopped), then read them intensively: every unknown word looked up, 3 cards. When a sitting finishes a chapter, write a 4-sentence Danish summary of that chapter in the notebook.

**Course books**

- **MTD pattern**: one of the fifteen portions you marked in B1-0002, in the textbook's own order: text with audio (blind first, twice), vocabulary to Anki, grammar notes copied, every exercise done and checked against the key. Shadow the chapter's text once. Write 8 sentences of your own on the chapter's theme; Correction protocol. Done when the chapter is complete and the 8 sentences are corrected.
- **GRAM pattern**: read the chapter section named in the step, copy every rule you did not already know into the notebook with the book's example and one of your own, then write the number of sentences stated and run the Correction protocol.
- **Andersen pattern**: the tale named in the step, in the original with modern spelling (andersenstories.com/da). Intensive: three readings (guess, look up, aloud), 20 words to the notebook, 15 cards. Then listen to a native reading on YouTube (the first result for the title plus "oplæsning") and shadow two paragraphs. One tracker line.

**Series and films**

- **Matador pattern** (Weeks 2–25; two steps for each episode). *Viewing*: watch the episode once with Danish subtitles, no pausing. *Scene work*, in the step that follows: take the scene that is running 20 minutes into the episode, from its first line, for 8–10 minutes. Rewatch it three times: with subtitles, without, then pausing to shadow ten lines. Write six sentences in Danish about what happened in the episode and one about a word or custom you did not know. Add 10 cards. In the tracker's Matador table note the episode number and one character you learned something about.
- **Krøniken pattern** (Weeks 26–33): watch the episode (about an hour) once with Danish subtitles, no pausing. Rewatch the five minutes that start 30 minutes in, pausing to shadow five lines. Write four sentences in Danish about what happened. No cards. In the tracker note the episode number and the year it is set in.
- **Casual-series pattern** (*Den store bagedyst*, *Bonderøven*, *Nak & Æd*, *Rita*): the episode named in the step, with Danish subtitles, extensive: no pausing, no notes. Episodes follow in order from the first one you watched. One tracker line.
- **Films** have their instructions in the step. Book → film: the film of a book comes within a week of the step that finishes the book.

**Books**

- **Book pattern** (the studied books: *Otto er et næsehorn*, *Orla Frøsnapper*, *Busters verden*, *Skammerens datter*, *Barndom*). The step title names the pages and the kind of sitting. Page numbers are for the editions in Appendix K; stop at the chapter end nearest the last page, and in another edition split the book in the same proportions.
  - *Intensive*: where an audiobook exists, listen to these pages first, without the text. Then read them with unknown words looked up, at most 15 of them written in the notebook, and 10 cards.
  - *Extensive*: read the pages with the audiobook playing, at most 4 lookups in the sitting (6 in *Barndom*), no cards.
  - *Reread*: the pages named, straight through with the audiobook, no dictionary.
  - *Review*: a 150-word review and a 3-minute oral retelling, both corrected (the step says so when the numbers differ).
- **Shelf pattern** (the free-reading shelf: *Lille Virgil*, *Hodja fra Pjort*, *Vildheks: Ildprøven*, *Skammerens søn*, *Intet*): extensive reading for pleasure. The audiobook plays, where there is one, while you follow the text; at most 3–5 lookups in the sitting; no cards; one tracker line: title and pages. Where the step says "part k of n", divide the book's page count by n, pencil the marks once, and read from one mark to the next, stopping at the nearest chapter end.

**Checks**

- **Weekly check**: the last step of each week that has no milestone. It passes when every step since the last check is ticked, Anki was cleared on 7 days of 7 and the dagbog was written on at least 6 days of 7.
- **Milestones** (Weeks 9, 18, 27) and the **exit check** (Weeks 34–35) add performance thresholds. A milestone that is too long for one sitting is split into parts, each its own step.

**December insert** (only for the part of this level that falls in December). DRTV puts its old julekalendere online from 1 November. On each day from 1 to 24 December, the first thing after the dailies, before the next numbered step, is that day's episode of *Nissebanden* (1984): episode 1 on 1 December and so on, about 25 minutes, Danish subtitles, extensive, one tracker line. If DRTV does not have *Nissebanden* that year, use *Jul på Slottet* (1986), and failing that *Tidsrejsen* (2014). These 24 episodes have no step numbers: log them in the tracker. After a missed day, watch two episodes the next day. While the insert runs, two things change in the numbered steps: tick the week's casual series step (*Den store bagedyst*, *Bonderøven* or *Nak & Æd*) with the note "julekalender" without watching it, and in the week's song step learn a julesang from Højskolesangbogen in place of the listed song, in this order: *Juletræet med sin pynt*, *Et barn er født i Betlehem*, *Dejlig er jorden*, *Glade jul, dejlige jul*.

## Appendix A: What changes at B1

1. **Danish about Danish.** Grammar help now comes first from sproget.dk and Dansk Sprognævn in Danish, with GRAM as the systematic backbone in English. Culture reading is in Danish (lex.dk, Danish Wikipedia, litteratursiden.dk). If a page is too hard, use text-to-speech and read for gist; do not switch to English.
2. **Native speed is the norm.** Genstart, TV Avisen and Matador are made for Danes. Extensive listening with no pausing is most of your listening time now; the podcast pattern (with transcripts) keeps the intensive side alive.
3. **Matador as spine.** Twenty-four episodes, one in each week from Week 2 to Week 25. It is the single most shared cultural reference in Denmark, its language is clear and slightly old-fashioned, and it teaches the century's history from the inside. The scene-shadowing part of the pattern is where the pronunciation gain is.
4. **Books, not readers.** Two Kirkegaard novels, Reuter, Kaaberbøl, Andersen, Ditlevsen. The book pattern's intensive-then-extensive rule matters more the longer the book: with *Skammerens datter* you go extensive after 40 pages, with the audiobook carrying you. 5. **Non-fiction is official.** The citizenship-test material is written in plain Danish about the things Danes assume everyone knows. Read all of it.
6. **The recurring steps** carry the A2 routines at higher volume: each week has four voice sessions, a monologue, a dictation, a bank text, three news articles and a song, every one of them its own step. They are nearly a third of the hours.
7. **Grammar goes systematic.** Every chapter of GRAM in order, with the exercises being your own sentences. By Week 25 nothing in the grammar should be new; from then on grammar work is only repair.
8. **Pronunciation moves to stress and timing.** Unit stress, stress on particles, unstressed function words and modal particles are what make you sound Danish rather than understandable. UDT and scene shadowing carry this.
9. **Prøve i Dansk 2 is the model exam.** Its reading, writing and oral formats shape the last five weeks; the official example papers are the exit check.
10. **Time.** The level is long. Count steps ticked, not weeks elapsed: a missed day moves everything back by a day, and nothing is skipped.
11. **A book, a series and something in your ears, every week** (README Method 6), all of it as numbered steps. Most of this is extensive and Casual: it is where the volume comes from, and it is what the intensive work is for.

## Appendix E: B1 exit reading test

**Bilfri søndag: en god idé eller bare besvær?**

Hver søndag i sommer har min hjemby, Randers, lukket centrum for biler mellem klokken ti og seksten. Idéen kom fra en gruppe forældre, som var trætte af, at deres børn ikke kunne cykle sikkert til svømmehallen. Kommunen sagde ja til et forsøg på tre måneder, og nu skal byrådet beslutte, om ordningen skal fortsætte.

Jeg synes, forsøget har været en succes. Da jeg gik gennem gågaden den første søndag, var der pludselig plads til alt det, der normalt bliver klemt væk: børn, der tegnede med kridt på vejen, ældre, der sad på bænke og snakkede, og en café, som havde sat borde ud, hvor der plejer at holde biler. Støjen var væk. Man kunne høre fuglene.

Selvfølgelig er der også ulemper. Butiksejerne i centrum har været delte. Nogle fortæller, at de har haft flere kunder end normalt, fordi folk bliver længere, når de ikke skal tænke på parkeringstiden. Andre siger, at kunder fra landsbyerne omkring byen er blevet væk, fordi de ikke gider parkere uden for centrum og gå det sidste stykke. Handelsstandsforeningen har derfor foreslået et kompromis: bilfri søndag én gang om måneden i stedet for hver uge.

Jeg forstår godt bekymringen, men jeg tror, den er overdrevet. I de andre byer, der har prøvet det samme, faldt omsætningen lidt de første uger, og derefter steg den igen. Folk vænner sig til det. Det tog også tid, før danskerne vænnede sig til rygeforbuddet på restauranter, og i dag er der ingen, der ønsker sig tilbage.

Det vigtigste argument er dog ikke handel, men sundhed og sikkerhed. Hvert år kommer børn til skade i trafikken i danske byer, og de fleste ulykker sker på korte ture i nærområdet. Hvis vi kan give børnene bare én dag om ugen, hvor de kan bevæge sig frit, har vi gjort noget, der er værd at gøre.

Derfor håber jeg, at byrådet stemmer for at fortsætte ordningen, og at de andre kommuner i Østjylland lader sig inspirere. Det koster næsten ingenting, det kræver ingen nye veje, og det giver os en by, som vi faktisk har lyst til at være i.
