# Level B1: Threshold

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A2 reached (step A2-255 ticked). Duolingo is retired. The README methods still apply, and the A2 weekly patterns (podcast pattern, dictation, AI voice sessions, monologues, dagbog) continue with the changes below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow the main points of clear standard Danish on familiar topics (news, a TV drama, a documentary, a conversation about work or opinions), handle almost any everyday situation including bureaucracy and the doctor, tell a story or explain an opinion in connected speech with reasons, read a children's novel, a young-adult novel, official information and newspaper articles, and write about 200 words of connected text in the right register (a formal letter to the municipality, an opinion piece, a personal narrative). Vocabulary about 4000 words. You know the complete grammar of Danish, including the full word-order system, both passives, phrasal verbs with their stress, indirect speech, conditionals, and the connector system, and your pronunciation has correct unit stress and rhythm. This is the level of Prøve i Dansk 2 and of the citizenship-test reading material.

**Time:** about 480 hours over 35 weeks, which is roughly 2 hours a day, 6 days a week. About half of that is the weekly rhythm (podcasts, news, voice sessions, writing, songs) and can be spread over commutes and evenings; the other half is desk work. If you only have 100 minutes a day, keep the order and let the level take 45 weeks.

**What changes at B1** (Appendix A): the culture track is now in Danish. The textbook is Danish-made. *Matador* is the television spine, one episode a week for 24 weeks. You read your first unabridged novels and Andersen in the original, and the official citizenship-test material becomes your non-fiction reader. Grammar is a systematic pass through *Danish: An Essential Grammar* from the first chapter to the last. News moves from easy-Danish articles to DR's normal news and, from Week 9, TV Avisen.

## The B1 weekly rhythm

Every week from Week 1 has four **rhythm steps**. They are listed each week as R1–R4 with only the week's specifics; the full definition is here.

- **R1 Listening**: one DIØ episode and one SDP episode with the A2 podcast pattern (blind twice, transcript, listen with text, 5-sentence summary scored, 60 seconds shadowed, 8 cards). Plus the week's news listening: *Ultra Nyt* twice (Weeks 1–8), *TV Avisen* twice (Week 9 on; use the player's Danish subtitles when the on-demand edition offers them, otherwise watch without and check your five headlines against dr.dk afterwards). From Week 5, *Genstart* (or the shorter *Tiden*) as stated. From Week 21 SDP is dropped and replaced by a second DIØ episode or a *Genstart*.
- **R2 Speaking**: four AI voice sessions of 15 minutes (the saved instruction now says "B1-niveau" and asks the assistant to use normal speed and to ask follow-up questions); one recorded monologue of 3–4 minutes on the next topic in Appendix F part 2, transcribed and corrected; one 2-minute dictation from a transcript source, logged.
- **R3 Writing and reading**: dagbog 6 days of 8–10 sentences, corrected on Sunday; one text from the writing bank (Appendix I) of about 200 words, corrected and filed in `writing/portfolio-B1.md`; three news articles (Ligetil until Week 5, then dr.dk/nyheder) read with at most 8 lookups each and a two-sentence Danish summary.
- **R4 Song**: one song learned by heart (Appendix J list, in order), all words looked up.

A week is complete when its rhythm steps and its content steps are ticked. Weekly checks ask for: Anki 7/7, dagbog 6/7, four voice sessions, one monologue, one dictation, one bank text, three articles, one song.

**Matador pattern** (Weeks 2–25): watch the episode once with Danish subtitles, no pausing. Then choose one 8–10 minute scene, rewatch it three times: with subtitles, without, then pausing to shadow ten lines. Write six sentences in Danish about what happened and one about a word or custom you did not know. Add 10 cards. In the tracker note the episode number and one character you learned something about.

**Book pattern**: intensive first read (unknown words looked up, at most 15 written per chapter, 20–30 cards a week), audiobook chapter listened to *before* reading where an audiobook exists; extensive second read of each finished book straight through; then a 150-word review and a 3-minute oral retelling, both corrected.

**GRAM pattern**: read the chapter section named in the step, copy every rule you did not already know into the notebook with the book's example and one of your own, then write the number of sentences stated and run the Correction protocol.

## Appendix A: What changes at B1

1. **Danish about Danish.** Grammar help now comes first from sproget.dk and Dansk Sprognævn in Danish, with GRAM as the systematic backbone in English. Culture reading is in Danish (lex.dk, Danish Wikipedia, litteratursiden.dk). If a page is too hard, use text-to-speech and read for gist; do not switch to English.
2. **Native speed is the norm.** Genstart, TV Avisen and Matador are made for Danes. Extensive listening with no pausing is most of your listening time now; the podcast pattern (with transcripts) keeps the intensive side alive.
3. **Matador as spine.** Twenty-four episodes, one a week. It is the single most shared cultural reference in Denmark, its language is clear and slightly old-fashioned, and it teaches the century's history from the inside. The scene-shadowing part of the pattern is where the pronunciation gain is.
4. **Books, not readers.** Two Kirkegaard novels, Reuter, Kaaberbøl, Andersen, Ditlevsen. The book pattern's intensive-then-extensive rule matters more the longer the book: with *Skammerens datter* you go extensive after 40 pages, with the audiobook carrying you.
5. **Non-fiction is official.** The citizenship-test material is written in plain Danish about the things Danes assume everyone knows. Read all of it.
6. **The rhythm steps** hold the A2 habits at higher volume: four voice sessions, a weekly monologue, a dictation, a bank text, three articles, a song. They are not optional; they are most of the hours.
7. **Grammar goes systematic.** Every chapter of GRAM in order, with the exercises being your own sentences. By Week 25 nothing in the grammar should be new; from then on grammar work is only repair.
8. **Pronunciation moves to rhythm.** Unit stress, stress on particles, unstressed function words and modal particles are what make you sound Danish rather than understandable. UDT and scene shadowing carry this.
9. **Prøve i Dansk 2 is the model exam.** Its reading, writing and oral formats shape the last five weeks; the official example papers are the exit check.
10. **Time.** The level is long. Count weeks completed, not weeks elapsed: a missed week is added at the end, not skipped.

## Appendix B: B1 grammar checklist

1. **Nouns**: plural classes, irregular plurals, Danish plural-only and singular-only nouns, genitive *-s* and its limits, *af/på/til* for possession.
2. **Demonstratives and determiners**: *den her/der, denne/dette/disse, sådan en, samme, begge, hver/hvert, enhver, al/alt/alle/hele, en del, masser af, de fleste*.
3. **Adjectives**: full agreement including after *noget/nogle/alle*, adjectives as nouns, indeclinables, *-t* adverbs, full comparison including *jo ... desto*, *lige så ... som*, *så ... som muligt*, *for ... til at*.
4. **Tenses**: present, past, perfect, pluperfect, the five future forms, future perfect (recognise), *have/være* choice with meaning differences.
5. **Modals**: every meaning of *skal, vil, kan, må, bør, tør, gider, behøver*; past forms for politeness and hypothesis.
6. **Conditionals**: real, unreal present, unreal past; inversion without *hvis*; *gid*; *som om* + past.
7. **Passive**: *-s* in present and past and after modals; *blive* in all tenses; *være* + participle as state; *få* + participle; agent *af*; *der* + passive.
8. ***-s* verbs**: *synes, findes, lykkes, trives, mindes, længes, skændes, slås, mødes, ses, tales ved, følges ad*.
9. **Reflexives and *sin***: *sig/sig selv/selv*, *sin* inside and across clauses, *hinanden*.
10. **Irregular verbs**: the A2 sixty plus: *bestå, forstå, opstå, tilbyde, forlade, overtage, undgå, indse, foreslå, beskrive, begynde, opholde sig, anse, afgøre, bidrage, udgive, vælge, svælge, skyde, smide, snyde, stryge, svinge, synke, tie, træde, tvinge, vige, vride, æde, gnide, glide, knibe, krybe, lyde, lyve, nyse, pibe, ryge, skride, skrige, slide, slippe, smyge, snige, spinde, sprække, stikke, stinke, strække, svide, sværge, tygge, vinde, virke* (checked on DDO; regular ones dropped).
11. **Phrasal verbs**: particle stress, object pronoun before the particle, 40 core particle verbs.
12. **Unit stress**: verb + bare object/complement loses stress; with article the verb keeps it; particles stressed; modal particles and function words unstressed.
13. **Main-clause word order**: the sentence scheme; central adverbs and their order; object pronouns before adverbs; fronting.
14. **Subordinate word order**: adverbs before the finite verb; *der* placeholder; indirect questions; reported speech with backshift; the *at*-clause exception.
15. **Cleft and emphasis**: *Det er/var X, der/som ...*; *Det er X, (at) ...*; heavy subjects with *det*.
16. **Relatives**: *som/der*, object omission, *hvis*, *hvilket*, *hvor*, *hvad der*, preposition stranding.
17. **Verb, adjective and noun + preposition**: sixty combinations (*tænke på, vente på, glæde sig til, passe på, holde af, holde op med, komme an på, regne med, stole på, tro på, tale om, bede om, spørge om, lede efter, se på, høre på, lytte til, kigge på, sørge for, gå ud fra, deltage i, bestå af, afhænge af, klage over, grine ad, blive ved med, begynde på, tage sig af, have råd til, have brug for, have lyst til, have tid til, være bange for, være glad for, være ked af, være sur på, være vred på, være stolt af, være træt af, være interesseret i, være god til, være vant til, være enig med/i, være uenig med/i, være sikker på, være tilfreds med, være nervøs for, være misundelig på, være forelsket i, være gift med, være afhængig af, interesse for, grund til, svar på, mangel på, brug for, lyst til, tid til, ret til, håb om*).
18. **Comparison advanced**: *jo ... desto/jo*, *stadig større*, *mere og mere*, *så vidt muligt*, *end nogensinde*.
19. **Coordination**: *både ... og, hverken ... eller, enten ... eller, ikke ... men, ikke kun ... men også*.
20. **Word formation**: linking *-s-/-e-*, stress and gender in compounds, nominalisation suffixes, prefixes.
21. **Orthography**: compounds in one word, *nogen/nogle, ad/af, sin/hans, ligge/lægge, sidde/sætte, hvis/hvem, ét/et, én/en*, capitalisation.
22. **Punctuation**: Sprognævnet's comma rules; comma after a fronted subordinate clause; between main clauses; not before *at* + infinitive; optional *startkomma* chosen consistently.
23. **Modal particles in use**: *jo, vel, da, nok, nu, lige, skam, godt, ellers, sgu, altså, egentlig*.
24. **Register**: formal letter conventions (*Til ..., Kære ..., Med venlig hilsen, Venlig hilsen, Kærlig hilsen*), *De/Dem/Deres* recognised, spoken reductions (*ik', ka', sku', ku', ha'*), casual intensifiers recognised (*mega, vildt, sindssygt, pisse-*).
25. **Numbers advanced**: *halvanden, et par, cirka, omkring, knap, godt*, decades, years read aloud, percentages, fractions, prices with *øre*, dates in letters.

## Appendix E: B1 exit reading test

**Bilfri søndag: en god idé eller bare besvær?**

Hver søndag i sommer har min hjemby, Randers, lukket centrum for biler mellem klokken ti og seksten. Idéen kom fra en gruppe forældre, som var trætte af, at deres børn ikke kunne cykle sikkert til svømmehallen. Kommunen sagde ja til et forsøg på tre måneder, og nu skal byrådet beslutte, om ordningen skal fortsætte.

Jeg synes, forsøget har været en succes. Da jeg gik gennem gågaden den første søndag, var der pludselig plads til alt det, der normalt bliver klemt væk: børn, der tegnede med kridt på vejen, ældre, der sad på bænke og snakkede, og en café, som havde sat borde ud, hvor der plejer at holde biler. Støjen var væk. Man kunne høre fuglene.

Selvfølgelig er der også ulemper. Butiksejerne i centrum har været delte. Nogle fortæller, at de har haft flere kunder end normalt, fordi folk bliver længere, når de ikke skal tænke på parkeringstiden. Andre siger, at kunder fra landsbyerne omkring byen er blevet væk, fordi de ikke gider parkere uden for centrum og gå det sidste stykke. Handelsstandsforeningen har derfor foreslået et kompromis: bilfri søndag én gang om måneden i stedet for hver uge.

Jeg forstår godt bekymringen, men jeg tror, den er overdrevet. I de andre byer, der har prøvet det samme, faldt omsætningen lidt de første uger, og derefter steg den igen. Folk vænner sig til det. Det tog også tid, før danskerne vænnede sig til rygeforbuddet på restauranter, og i dag er der ingen, der ønsker sig tilbage.

Det vigtigste argument er dog ikke handel, men sundhed og sikkerhed. Hvert år kommer børn til skade i trafikken i danske byer, og de fleste ulykker sker på korte ture i nærområdet. Hvis vi kan give børnene bare én dag om ugen, hvor de kan bevæge sig frit, har vi gjort noget, der er værd at gøre.

Derfor håber jeg, at byrådet stemmer for at fortsætte ordningen, og at de andre kommuner i Østjylland lader sig inspirere. Det koster næsten ingenting, det kræver ingen nye veje, og det giver os en by, som vi faktisk har lyst til at være i.

*Mette Kristiansen, Randers*
