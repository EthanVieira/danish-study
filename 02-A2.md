# Level A2: Waystage

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A1 reached (step A1-0287 ticked) and [README.md](README.md) methods still in use.

**Goal at exit:** you can handle routine situations (travel, shops, post office, pharmacy, housing, doctor) and short social exchanges, describe your background, surroundings, plans and past experiences in connected sentences, understand the main points of slow clear speech and learner podcasts on familiar topics, read short simple texts, easy news and a short children's novel, and write connected texts of about 150 words (emails, descriptions, simple narratives). Vocabulary about 2000 words. You know the whole core grammar of Danish, including subordinate clauses, comparison, reflexives, relative clauses and the simple passive, and your pronunciation is stable enough that you are understood on the first try.

**Time:** about 400 hours over 20 weeks. The 613 numbered steps take about 330 h: 15–23 h in a teaching week (Week 1 has 11 h after its 3 h of setup), about 10 h in each of the two milestone weeks (6 and 12) and about 5 h of input steps in each of the two exit weeks. The two dailies, Anki and the dagbog, take a little under 25 minutes a day, every day: about 55 h. The milestone and exit tests take the rest. With six study days a week that is about 3 h 20 min a day. If you have less time, stretch the calendar and keep the order.

**What changes at A2** (read Appendix A first): the weight shifts from courses towards input. Pimsleur is finished. Two learner podcasts, DR's easy-Danish articles, DR's children's news and a tween drama run through the level as numbered steps. You read your first real Danish books. Speaking practice moves from repeating to producing, with AI voice conversations and recorded monologues.

**How a day works at A2:** do the two dailies, then the next unticked step. The dailies carried over from A1 are Anki, now with 20 new cards a day after all due reviews, and the dagbog, now 6–8 sentences a day. Nothing else recurs outside the numbered steps. The week's dagbog entries are corrected in a Dagbog correction step. Reading, series and listening are scheduled as steps in every week, milestone and exit weeks included: **Reading** steps name a book from the free-reading shelf and the part to read, **Series** steps name the episodes (*Klassen* throughout, with *Motor Mille* in Block 1 and *Kender du typen?* in Block 3), and **Listening** steps name the audio to replay or the album to hear. You never choose what to read, watch or hear, or when. Book → film at A2: *Vitello* → *Vitello* (2018), *Gummi-Tarzan* → *Gummi Tarzan* (1981), Peter Madsen's *Valhalla* albums → *Valhalla* (1986).

## Patterns

A step that names a pattern is done exactly as the pattern says, and it is finished when the pattern's last instruction is done. The patterns say how to do an activity; the steps say when, and with what.

**Complete Danish four-step pattern (CD):** (a) dialogue LRS, (b) grammar and exercises, (c) vocabulary and shadowing, (d) 8 sentences corrected. It continues exactly as at A1 and is not restated per step.

**Colloquial Danish pattern (COLL units 10–18):** two steps per unit. (a) LRS cycle on all dialogues plus all unit vocabulary into Anki; (b) grammar notes copied with own examples, all exercises done and checked, 8 own sentences corrected. Units 1–9 are not studied, because they repeat A1 ground (the extra Reading steps of Block 1 take their place); if a unit 10+ dialogue relies on a name or word from them, look it up in the book's glossary.

**Podcast pattern (DIØ and SDP):** the step names the episode. DIØ episodes are numbered in the order you study them at A2: DIØ episode 1 is the next episode in order after the ones you used at A1 (A1-0209 and A1-0273), and each later step takes the next one. SDP episodes count from the earliest episode. LRS stages 1–3, then write a 5-sentence English summary *before* reading the transcript, then check it against the transcript and score yourself out of 5. Add 8 cards. From Week 7, add stage 4 (shadow one 60-second segment) as well. Finished when the summary is scored and the 8 cards are added. **Test episodes:** the checks that need an episode "you have not heard" (A2-0174, A2-0589) take it from the far end of the back catalogue (episode 60 or later for DIØ; the same rule for SDP), never the next one in order. Those episodes never appear in a Listening step.

**Short Stories chapter pattern (SSD stories 1–4):** one chapter per step. Listen to the audiobook chapter once blind, read the chapter looking up every unknown word not in the book's glossary, listen again with the text, answer the chapter's comprehension questions and check. Add the number of cards the step names. After the last chapter come an extensive step (as A2-0077) and a retelling step (as A2-0081).

**Short Stories second-half pattern (SSD stories 5–8), extensive first:** three steps. (1) Extensive pass, 30 min: play the whole story's audio at normal speed while reading along, no stopping and no dictionary, then answer the book's comprehension questions from memory. (2) Intensive pass, 45 min: take the one chapter you understood least and do it by the chapter pattern (listen blind, read with lookups, listen with the text), with at most 10 cards; then check the questions again. (3) Retelling as A2-0081.

**Reading pattern (free-reading shelf):** 45 minutes with the named book, read straight on. No dictionary, no notebook, no cards; look up at most 3–5 words that keep coming back. If the book has an audiobook, read along with it. Read the named part. If you finish it before the time is up, read it again from its start until the time is up; if the time runs out first, stop and carry on from there in the book's next sitting (a magazine issue you do not finish in its last sitting stays unfinished). One tracker line: title and pages. Keep a shelf list in the tracker as well: title, date started, date finished. The shelf books should be ones where you know about 95 percent of the words. If a *Dingo* reader has more than 3 unknown words on a page, take it back and borrow one from the LIX band below; it keeps its number.

**Valhalla pattern:** one of Peter Madsen's *Valhalla* comic albums in a single 30-minute sitting, starting with album 1, *Ulven er løs* (1979). Extensive rules: no cards, no notebook, at most 5 lookups. The pictures carry the story; the speech bubbles are colloquial spoken Danish, which the graded readers lack. If the library has no *Valhalla* albums, read a further issue of *Anders And & Co.* in each of these steps instead.

**Klassen pattern:** the step names three new episodes; watch them with Danish subtitles, straight through. Then rewatch only the one you understood least, without subtitles. Write 5 phrases you would use yourself. Episode numbers count straight on from season 1, episode 1, across the seasons. In Block 3 a *Klassen* step has two new episodes with Danish subtitles and no rewatch; in Weeks 19 and 20 it has three and no rewatch. One tracker line per episode.

**Motor Mille pattern:** one episode of *Motor Mille og Børnebanden* (Ramasjang), in order from the oldest episode on DRTV: watch it once with Danish subtitles, no tasks. One tracker line.

**Kender du typen? pattern:** your first adult Danish TV: *Kender du typen?* (DR1, experts guess who lives in a home from its furniture, food and hobbies). Two whole episodes, about 60 minutes, with Danish subtitles and no pausing: the two oldest episodes on DRTV that you have not seen (DRTV rotates which episodes are available, so take whatever season is up). Afterwards write one Danish sentence per episode. If the programme is not on DRTV, watch one episode of *Hammerslag* (DR1, house buying) in each of these steps instead. Both shows repeat the vocabulary of homes, family and hobbies (Appendix C domains 2, 11 and 16).

**Ultra Nyt pattern:** the newest episode of *Ultra Nyt*, DR's children's news: watch it first with Danish subtitles, then a second time without. Write its three headlines in Danish.

**Ligetil pattern:** one article from dr.dk/ligetil, the newest one you have not read. Read it once with text-to-speech on, once without. Look up at most 5 words and add the number of cards the step names. Note the headline in the notebook with one sentence in Danish about it.

**Listening pattern:** a Listening step replays audio you have already studied or read: 35 minutes at 1.0x speed, with no pausing, no tasks and no notes. A walk or the commute is fine. Play the named items in the order given. If they end before the time is up, start again from the first item; stop when the time is up. Sources: the *Dansk her og nu* podcast (podcast.danskherognu.dk), the CD and COLL dialogue audio, DIØ and SDP episodes you have studied, the SSD audiobook, the free Bostrup audio, and the *Vitello* and *Gummi-Tarzan* audiobooks. If a named audiobook is not available to you, play the SSD story you finished most recently instead.

**Album pattern:** one whole named album (Spotify, Apple Music or YouTube), 45 minutes, straight through, with the lyrics open if you can (Genius, or search the title plus "tekst"). No tasks. If the album ends early, start it again from the first track until the time is up.

**Voice-session pattern:** 10 minutes of spoken Danish with the AI voice mode, using your saved instruction (A2-0007), on the topic the step names. Speak in full sentences; if you cannot find a word, describe it in Danish rather than switching to English. Do not stop to fix yourself. Afterwards, in text, ask for the list of errors and copy any recurring one into the notebook.

**Monologue pattern:** one Recording protocol monologue of 2–3 minutes on the named topic from Appendix F (part 2), transcribed and corrected. Finished when the recording and the corrections are done.

**Dictation pattern:** take the 90 seconds of audio the step names. Play it in 5-second chunks and write every word. Compare with the transcript, count wrong or missing words, and record the percentage in `writing/dictations.md`. Rewrite each wrong word 3 times. Two thresholds: the last two of the seven dictations must be at or under 15 percent (counted in A2-0611); the exit test dictation (A2-0589) must be at or under 12 percent.

**Song pattern:** each song is learned by heart in three sittings of 15 minutes. Sitting 1: find the lyrics and a recording, read the lyrics and look up every word, then listen twice with the text. Sitting 2: sing along with the recording until you need the text for only a few lines. Sitting 3: sing along once, then sing the song from memory without the recording. Finished when the named verses are sung from memory.

**Dagbog-correction pattern:** run the whole week's dagbog entries through the Correction protocol in one go and copy the corrections into the notebook by hand.

## Appendix A: What changes at A2

1. **Input becomes the main course.** At A1 the textbook was the meal and input was dessert. At A2 about half of all your hours (roughly 200 of 400) go to listening, reading and watching: the Reading, Series and Listening steps, the podcasts, the books, the series and films, Ligetil and *Ultra Nyt*. The other half goes to textbooks, grammar, drills, writing, speaking and Anki. Expect to feel that you understand less than you "should"; that feeling is what growth sounds like.
2. **A book, a series and something in your ears, every week.** A shelf book is always open, a series is always running, and replays and albums keep Danish in your ears (Method 6). These Reading, Series and Listening steps have no cards and no tasks, so they are the easiest steps of the week and the first ones a tired week tempts you to skip. Do not skip them: they are steps like any other, and the weekly checks count them.
3. **Two podcasts, one pattern.** *Dansk i ørerne* (slower, shorter, about Danish life) and *Simple Danish Podcast* (two hosts, conversational). Blind first, always. The transcript is a check, not a crutch.
4. **Audiobook plus text.** For *Short Stories in Danish*, listen to a chapter before reading it in stories 1–4; from story 5, read and listen to the whole story first and study only the hardest chapter. Always finish with a full-speed listen while reading. This is the fastest way to close the gap between your reading and your listening.
5. **Dictation.** Seven Dictation steps from Week 10. It is the most honest listening test there is and it fixes spelling as a side effect. Log the percentages; watch them fall.
6. **Talking to a machine.** The Voice session steps are for fluency, not accuracy. Do not stop to fix yourself. Get the error list afterwards. If a session goes badly, it still counts; a bad session is practice.
7. **Read whole books, two ways.** Two *Vitello* books, two graded readers and *Gummi-Tarzan* are the studied books: intensive on the first pass, extensive on the second, and always a summary afterwards. The free-reading shelf is the other way: easy books, comics and audiobooks read only for the story, with no cards and no summary. You need both, and the shelf is where the volume comes from. When a book has a film, watch it within a week of finishing the book.
8. **Grammar is now about clauses.** A1 was the sentence; A2 is how sentences join. The three most important things at this level are subordinate word order, *som/der*, and tense choice in narrative. The checks look for them specifically.
9. **Songs continue** because they lodge whole phrases with correct stress. Kim Larsen is the A2 songbook; every Dane can sing along, and so will you. You learn fifteen songs by heart, each in three short sittings, and hear twenty whole albums in Listening steps.
10. **Culture reading is still in English** where it has to be (Kierkegaard, Grundtvig, Jante), but the tracker now has Danish books actually read in Danish, and every film at this level is watched with Danish subtitles. From B1 the culture track is in Danish only.

## Appendix B: A2 grammar checklist

1. **Comparison**: *-ere/-est*, *mere/mest*, irregulars (*god/bedre/bedst, gammel/ældre/ældst, stor/større/størst, lille/mindre/mindst, mange/flere/flest, meget/mere/mest, lang/længere/længst, ung/yngre/yngst, dårlig/værre/værst, få/færre/færrest*), *end*, *lige så ... som*, *den/det/de ...-este*.
2. **Subordinate clauses**: *at, fordi, når, da, hvis, om, selvom, mens, før/inden, efter at, så, indtil, siden*; sentence adverb before the verb; inversion in the main clause after a fronted subordinate clause.
3. **Future**: present + time word, *skal*, *vil*, *kommer til at*, *bliver*.
4. **Reflexives**: *mig, dig, sig, os, jer, sig*; reflexive verbs (*glæde sig, skynde sig, sætte sig, lægge sig, kede sig, føle sig, gifte sig*); *selv*; *hinanden*; *sin/sit/sine* vs *hans/hendes/deres*.
5. **Relative clauses**: *der/som* (subject), *som* or nothing (object), *hvis* (whose), preposition at the end.
6. **Place adverb pairs**: *hjem/hjemme, ud/ude, ind/inde, op/oppe, ned/nede, over/ovre, hen/henne, bort/borte, frem/fremme*.
7. **Passive**: *-s* passive (habitual, written); *blive* + participle (events); *af* for the agent.
8. **Time prepositions**: *i, om, for ... siden, siden, indtil, i løbet af, på, til*; *sidste/næste/hver* without preposition.
9. **Modal particles** (recognise): *jo, vel, da, nok, lige, godt, nu, vist, bare, altså*.
10. **Sixty irregular verbs**, four forms each: *være, have, blive, få, gå, komme, se, sige, gøre, vide, tage, give, stå, sidde, ligge, lægge, sætte, finde, holde, falde, lade, bede, bringe, bære, binde, bide, bryde, byde, drikke, drive, dø, flyve, fryse, græde, gribe, hedde, hjælpe, kunne, skulle, ville, måtte, burde, turde, le, lide, løbe, nyde, ride, ryge, skrive, skyde, skære, slå, sove, springe, spørge, stjæle, synge, træffe, vinde*.
11. **Indefinite pronouns and quantifiers**: *nogen/noget/nogle, ingen/intet/ingenting, man/en/ens, alle/al/alt/hele, hver/hvert, begge, anden/andet/andre, samme, sådan*.
12. **Formal subjects**: *det* (infinitive/clause subjects, weather, impersonal), *der* (indefinite real subject).
13. **Tense in narrative**: past for the story, perfect for results and experiences, pluperfect for earlier past.
14. **Compounds and suffixes**: one-word compounds with gender from the last part; *-hed, -else, -ning, -skab, -er, -ig/-lig, u-*.
15. **Infinitive**: *at* + infinitive, bare infinitive after modals and *lade/se/høre*, *for at*, *være ved at, komme til at, have lyst til at*.
16. **Object pronoun and adverb placement**: *Jeg kender ham ikke* (pronoun object before *ikke*); *Jeg kender ikke manden*.
17. **Imperative and polite requests**: *Sæt dig! Skynd dig!*; *Kan/Vil du ...?*, *Kunne du ...?*, *Må jeg bede om ...?*, *Jeg vil gerne ...*.
18. **Adjective as noun and *-e* plural of adjectives after *de/alle***: *de gamle, alle de små*.
19. **Question tags and short answers**: *ikke?*, *vel?*, *Ja, det gør jeg. / Nej, det har jeg ikke. / Jo, det kan jeg godt.*
20. **Numbers in real use**: prices with *kroner og øre*, years (*nittenhundredeogfireogfirs*, or *1984* read as *nitten fireogfirs*), phone numbers in pairs, dates, percentages (*procent*), fractions (*en halv, en tredjedel, en kvart*).

## Appendix E: A2 exit reading test

**En tur til Bornholm**

Sidste sommer tog Anna og hendes kæreste, Mikkel, på ferie til Bornholm. De havde aldrig været der før, men Annas kollega havde fortalt, at øen var meget smuk. De tog toget fra København til Ystad i Sverige og derefter færgen til Rønne. Turen tog cirka tre timer.

De boede på en lille campingplads ved stranden, tæt på Dueodde, som er kendt for sit hvide sand. Hver morgen stod de tidligt op og cyklede rundt på øen. Mikkel ville gerne se rundkirkerne, som er over 800 år gamle, mens Anna hellere ville besøge de små fiskerbyer med røgerier, hvor man kan købe røget sild.

En dag regnede det hele dagen. De blev i teltet og spillede kort, og om aftenen gik de på en café i Svaneke. Der mødte de et ældre par fra Tyskland, som kom til Bornholm hvert år. Parret anbefalede dem at tage til Hammershus, en gammel borgruin i den nordlige del af øen. Næste dag tog de derhen. Udsigten over havet var fantastisk, og Anna tog mange billeder.

Efter en uge skulle de hjem igen. På færgen sagde Mikkel, at det havde været den bedste ferie, han nogensinde havde haft. Anna var enig, men hun var også lidt træt af at sove i telt. "Næste gang lejer vi et sommerhus," sagde hun og smilede.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk, hele sætninger):

1. Hvornår rejste Anna og Mikkel til Bornholm?
2. Havde de været på Bornholm før?
3. Hvordan kom de til Bornholm?
4. Hvor boede de?
5. Hvad ville Mikkel gerne se?
6. Hvad ville Anna hellere lave?
7. Hvad lavede de den dag, det regnede?
8. Hvem anbefalede dem at tage til Hammershus?
9. Hvad er Hammershus?
10. Hvorfor vil Anna leje et sommerhus næste gang?

**Key:** 1. Sidste sommer. 2. Nej, aldrig. 3. Med tog til Ystad og så med færge til Rønne. 4. På en lille campingplads ved stranden tæt på Dueodde. 5. Rundkirkerne. 6. Besøge de små fiskerbyer med røgerier (og købe røget sild). 7. De blev i teltet og spillede kort, og om aftenen gik de på café i Svaneke. 8. Et ældre par fra Tyskland. 9. En gammel borgruin i den nordlige del af Bornholm. 10. Fordi hun var træt af at sove i telt.

If the library lacks a named title, any single title not named in this file replaces it, or the anthology, read in the same number of sittings. In print; in every Danish library; check eReolen, eReolen Go and Mofibo/Nextory for audiobook editions. The animated film *Vitello* (2018) is based on the books (A2-0306).
- **Forlaget Bostrup** *Letlæste bøger for udenlandske studerende* (lisebostrup.dk/letlaesteboger): *Sølvmønten* (H.C. Andersen retold, tested at modul 2–3; studied from A2-0320), *Charleston-pigen Anne* (studied from A2-0495) and *Farvel og tak* (Maria Helleberg and Lise Bostrup, modul 2–3), *Nissen hos den gamle købmand* (Andersen retold), *Amalie og skelettyvene* (a shelf book in Week 12). All but the last have free audio read by Lise Bostrup on the site. Order through the site's contact form or any Danish bookshop.
- **Gummi-Tarzan**, Ole Lund Kirkegaard, 1975 (Gyldendal hardback ISBN 9788701239813, 128 pages, large type). Audiobook editions include one read by Peter Frödin and an archive recording of the author reading (released 2009; Kirkegaard died in 1979); check eReolen, Mofibo and Nextory for which is available and its running time. In the tracker as the first unabridged Danish book. Film: *Gummi Tarzan* (1981, Søren Kragh-Jacobsen), A2-0506.
- **H.C. Andersen texts**: for modern spelling use andersenstories.com/da (all tales, free). The H.C. Andersen Centre's registry at andersen.sdu.dk/vaerk/register/eventyr.html indexes all 213 tales and links to the original-spelling texts on adl.dk (*Prindsessen paa Ærten*, *Keiserens nye Klæder*); use those for curiosity only at this level. *Prinsessen på ærten* (1835) is about 300 words; *Kejserens nye klæder* (1837) about 1300.
- **DR Ligetil**: dr.dk/ligetil, short easy-Danish articles with text-to-speech and word help, plus picture stories. Free.
- **Dansk i ørerne** (host Sofie Lindholm): over 110 episodes of 15–20 minutes, and new ones are still added (check danskioererne.dk for the current release pace); free transcripts of every episode at danskioererne.dk; in all podcast apps. Start from episode 1 and go in order; keep episodes from 60 onward for the tests.
- **Simple Danish Podcast** (Denmark and Me): free transcripts and exercises for each episode at denmarkandme.com/podcast; on all podcast apps and YouTube.
- **DRTV**: *Klassen* (DR Ultra, dr.dk/drtv/serie/klassen_6463; 21 seasons of roughly 10-minute episodes about a school class; start at season 1; toggle Danish subtitles in the player), *Ultra Nyt* (DR's news for children, Monday to Thursday, 8–11 minutes), *Motor Mille og Børnebanden* (Ramasjang, 17-minute episodes), *Kender du typen?* and *Hammerslag* (DR1 lifestyle and housing programmes; which seasons are on DRTV changes, so take whichever is up). Geoblocking as in A1.
- **Library services**: eReolen (ereolen.dk), eReolen Go (ereolengo.dk) and Filmstriben (filmstriben.dk), free with a Danish library card (get one with MitID). Monthly loan limits are set by your municipality; reserve popular audiobooks early. See the README, Method 6.
- **Free-reading shelf** (the shelf list under Patterns says which title is read when): Gyldendal's *Mini Dingo* and *Dingo* easy readers (every public library; ask for the LIX band above your A1 reader); *Rasmus Klump* (Carla and Vilhelm Hansen); the remaining *Vitello* titles; Bostrup's *Amalie og skelettyvene*; *Anders And & Co.* (the weekly Danish Donald Duck magazine; libraries keep back issues); Peter Madsen's *Valhalla* albums 1–5 (from 1979; album 1 *Ulven er løs*; ask your library). Stretch: *Harry Potter og De Vises Sten* (translated by Hanna Lützen), with Roald Dahl's *Charlie og chokoladefabrikken* as the fallback; check eReolen and eReolen Go for the e-book and the audiobook.
- **Films**: *Jungledyret Hugo* (1993 film) is on Netflix Denmark and rentable from the Viaplay Store or Google Play. *Krummerne* (1991) is on Nordisk Film+ and TV 2 Play and rentable from the Viaplay Store, Apple TV and SF Anytime. *Far til fire* (both the 1950s–70s classics and the 2005 onward reboot) are on Nordisk Film+ and TV 2 Play and rentable on SF Anytime. For *Vitello* (2018), *Ternet Ninja* (2018), *Gummi Tarzan* (1981) and *Valhalla* (1986), check Filmstriben first, then JustWatch (Denmark); streaming catalogues change often. Nordisk Film+ (about 69 kr a month) covers most of the Danish film canon and is worth a subscription from here to C2. Disney+ Denmark carries Danish audio and Danish subtitles on all Disney and Pixar animation.
- **Music**: Kim Larsen's *Værsgo* (1973, A2-0478) and the twenty albums of the album Listening steps (each step names its album: Kim Larsen and Gasolin', Shu-bi-dua, TV-2, Rasmus Seebach), on Spotify/Apple Music/YouTube. If your service lacks a named album, take the same artist's next album by release year. Lyrics on Genius or by searching the title plus "tekst". *Højskolesangbogen* (19th edition, 2020, Folkehøjskolernes Forening) is in print; hojskolesangbogen.dk has song information and many lyrics; *Der er et yndigt land* and *I Danmark er jeg født* are in it.
