# Level A2: Waystage

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A1 reached (step A1-154 ticked) and [README.md](README.md) methods still in use.

**Goal at exit:** you can handle routine situations (travel, shops, post office, pharmacy, housing, doctor) and short social exchanges, describe your background, surroundings, plans and past experiences in connected sentences, understand the main points of slow clear speech and learner podcasts on familiar topics, read short simple texts, easy news and a short children's novel, and write connected texts of about 150 words (emails, descriptions, simple narratives). Vocabulary about 2000 words. You know the whole core grammar of Danish, including subordinate clauses, comparison, reflexives, relative clauses and the simple passive, and your pronunciation is stable enough that you are understood on the first try.

**Time:** about 400 hours over 20 weeks: about 2 h 40 min a day, 6 days a week (steps, Anki, dagbog and the other habits), plus about 40 minutes a day in the ear lane on the commute and the book lane at bedtime. The numbered steps account for about 220 h; Anki and dagbog take about 60 h, the ear and book lanes about 80 h, and the other weekly habits (AI voice, Ligetil) and the checks the rest. If you have less time, stretch the calendar and keep the order.

**What changes at A2** (read Appendix A first): the weight shifts from courses towards input. Pimsleur is finished. Two learner podcasts, DR's easy-Danish articles, DR's children's news and a tween drama become weekly habits. You read your first real Danish books. Speaking practice moves from repeating to producing, with an AI voice conversation habit and weekly recorded monologues.

**Method 6 at A2:** the three lanes started at A1 keep running every week, milestone and exit weeks included. **Book lane:** a book from the free-reading shelf (SHELF below) is always open, read at bedtime and in the book-lane sittings, with no cards. **Series lane:** Block 1 runs *Klassen* and *Motor Mille*, Block 2 *Klassen*, Block 3 *Klassen* and *Kender du typen?* (or *Hammerslag*), and *Klassen* carries on through the exit weeks. **Ear lane:** the commute slot replays podcast episodes and audiobooks you have already studied or read (EAR below). Book → film at A2: *Vitello* → *Vitello* (2018), *Gummi-Tarzan* → *Gummi Tarzan* (1981), Peter Madsen's *Valhalla* albums → *Valhalla* (1986).

## Block 1: Weeks 1–5

The Complete Danish four-step pattern (a) dialogue LRS, (b) grammar and exercises, (c) vocabulary and shadowing, (d) 8 sentences corrected, continues exactly as at A1 and is not restated per step.

**Book-lane sitting (extensive reading):** 45 minutes with a book from the free-reading shelf (SHELF), read straight on. Choose books where you know about 95 percent of the words: if a page has more than 3 unknown words, put the book back and pick an easier one. No dictionary, no notebook, no cards; look up at most 3–5 words that keep coming back. If the book has an audiobook, read along with it. When you finish a book, start the next one the following day. One tracker line: title and pages. (These sittings replace a fast pass of *Colloquial Danish* units 1–9, which repeated A1 ground.)

**Dansk i ørerne weekly pattern (DIØ):** pick the next episode in order after the ones you used at A1 (A1-122 and A1-146). LRS stages 1–3, then write a 5-sentence English summary *before* reading the transcript, then check it against the transcript and score yourself out of 5. Add 8 cards. From Week 7, add stage 4 (shadow one 60-second segment) as well. **Test episodes:** the checks that need an episode "you have not heard" (A2-082, A2-246) take it from the far end of the back catalogue (episode 60 or later for DIØ; the same rule for SDP), never the next one in order. Do not play those episodes in the ear lane.

**Klassen weekly pattern:** three new episodes per week with Danish subtitles, straight through. Then rewatch only the one you understood least, without subtitles. Write 5 phrases you would use yourself. In Block 1 the series-lane step also has one episode of *Motor Mille og Børnebanden* (Ramasjang): watch it once with Danish subtitles, no tasks.

## Appendix A: What changes at A2

1. **Input becomes the main course.** At A1 the textbook was the meal and input was dessert. At A2 about half of all your hours (roughly 200 of 400) go to listening, reading and watching: the three lanes, the podcasts, the books, the series and films, Ligetil and *Ultra Nyt*. The other half goes to textbooks, grammar, drills, writing, speaking and Anki. Expect to feel that you understand less than you "should"; that feeling is what growth sounds like.
2. **Three lanes, never empty.** A shelf book is always open, a series is always running, and the commute is for listening (Method 6). The lanes have no cards and no tasks, so they are the easiest part of the week to keep and the first thing a tired week tempts you to drop. Do not drop them: the weekly checks count them.
3. **Two podcasts, one pattern.** *Dansk i ørerne* (slower, shorter, about Danish life) and *Simple Danish Podcast* (two hosts, conversational). Blind first, always. The transcript is a check, not a crutch.
4. **Audiobook plus text.** For *Short Stories in Danish*, listen to a chapter before reading it in stories 1–4; from story 5, read and listen to the whole story first and study only the hardest chapter. Always finish with a full-speed listen while reading. This is the fastest way to close the gap between your reading and your listening.
5. **Dictation.** Weekly from Week 10. It is the most honest listening test there is and it fixes spelling as a side effect. Log the percentages; watch them fall.
6. **Talking to a machine.** The AI voice sessions are for fluency, not accuracy. Do not stop to fix yourself. Get the error list afterwards. If a session goes badly, it still counts; a bad session is practice.
7. **Read whole books, two ways.** Two *Vitello* books, two graded readers and *Gummi-Tarzan* are the studied books: intensive on the first pass, extensive on the second, and always a summary afterwards. The free-reading shelf is the other way: easy books, comics and audiobooks read only for the story, with no cards and no summary. You need both, and the shelf is where the volume comes from. When a book has a film, watch it within a week of finishing the book.
8. **Grammar is now about clauses.** A1 was the sentence; A2 is how sentences join. The three most important things at this level are subordinate word order, *som/der*, and tense choice in narrative. The checks look for them specifically.
9. **Songs continue** because they lodge whole phrases with correct stress. Kim Larsen is the A2 songbook; every Dane can sing along, and so will you. You learn one song a week by heart and hear one whole album a week in the ear lane.
10. **Culture reading is still in English** where it has to be (Kierkegaard, Grundtvig, Jante), but the tracker now has Danish books actually read in Danish, and every film at this level is watched with Danish subtitles. From B1 the culture track is in Danish only.

## Appendix B: A2 grammar checklist

1. **Comparison**: *-ere/-est*, *mere/mest*, irregulars (*god/bedre/bedst, gammel/ældre/ældst, stor/større/størst, lille/mindre/mindst, mange/flere/flest, meget/mere/mest, lang/længere/længst, ung/yngre/yngst, dårlig/værre/værst, få/færre/færrest*), *end*, *lige så ... som*, *den/det/de ...-este*.
2. **Subordinate clauses**: *at, fordi, når, da, hvis, om, selvom, mens, før/inden, efter at, så, indtil, siden*; sentence adverb before the verb; inversion in the main clause after a fronted subordinate clause.
3. **Future**: present + time word, *skal*, *vil*, *kommer til at*, *bliver*.
4. **Reflexives**: *mig, dig, sig, os, jer, sig*; reflexive verbs (*glæde sig, skynde sig, sætte sig, lægge sig, kede sig, føle sig, gifte sig*); *selv*; *hinanden*; *sin/sit/sine* vs *hans/hendes/deres*.
5. **Relative clauses**: *der/som* (subject), *som* or nothing (object), *hvis* (whose), preposition at the end.
6. **Place adverb pairs**: *hjem/hjemme, ud/ude, ind/inde, op/oppe, ned/nede, over/ovre, hen/henne, bort/borte, frem/fremme*.
7. **Passive**: *-s* passive (habitual, written); *blive* + participle (events); *af* for the agent.
8. **Time prepositions**: *i, om, for ... siden, siden, indtil, i løbet af, på, til*; *sidste/næste/hver* without preposition.
9. **Modal particles** (recognise): *jo, vel, da, nok, lige, godt, nu, vist, bare, altså*.
10. **Sixty irregular verbs**, four forms each: *være, have, blive, få, gå, komme, se, sige, gøre, vide, tage, give, stå, sidde, ligge, lægge, sætte, finde, holde, falde, lade, bede, bringe, bære, binde, bide, bryde, byde, drikke, drive, dø, flyve, fryse, græde, gribe, hedde, hjælpe, kunne, skulle, ville, måtte, burde, turde, le, lide, løbe, nyde, ride, ryge, skrive, skyde, skære, slå, sove, springe, spørge, stjæle, synge, træffe, vinde*.
11. **Indefinite pronouns and quantifiers**: *nogen/noget/nogle, ingen/intet/ingenting, man/en/ens, alle/al/alt/hele, hver/hvert, begge, anden/andet/andre, samme, sådan*.
12. **Formal subjects**: *det* (infinitive/clause subjects, weather, impersonal), *der* (indefinite real subject).
13. **Tense in narrative**: past for the story, perfect for results and experiences, pluperfect for earlier past.
14. **Compounds and suffixes**: one-word compounds with gender from the last part; *-hed, -else, -ning, -skab, -er, -ig/-lig, u-*.
15. **Infinitive**: *at* + infinitive, bare infinitive after modals and *lade/se/høre*, *for at*, *være ved at, komme til at, have lyst til at*.
16. **Object pronoun and adverb placement**: *Jeg kender ham ikke* (pronoun object before *ikke*); *Jeg kender ikke manden*.
17. **Imperative and polite requests**: *Sæt dig! Skynd dig!*; *Kan/Vil du ...?*, *Kunne du ...?*, *Må jeg bede om ...?*, *Jeg vil gerne ...*.
18. **Adjective as noun and *-e* plural of adjectives after *de/alle***: *de gamle, alle de små*.
19. **Question tags and short answers**: *ikke?*, *vel?*, *Ja, det gør jeg. / Nej, det har jeg ikke. / Jo, det kan jeg godt.*
20. **Numbers in real use**: prices with *kroner og øre*, years (*nittenhundredeogfireogfirs*, or *1984* read as *nitten fireogfirs*), phone numbers in pairs, dates, percentages (*procent*), fractions (*en halv, en tredjedel, en kvart*).

## Appendix E: A2 exit reading test

**En tur til Bornholm**

Sidste sommer tog Anna og hendes kæreste, Mikkel, på ferie til Bornholm. De havde aldrig været der før, men Annas kollega havde fortalt, at øen var meget smuk. De tog toget fra København til Ystad i Sverige og derefter færgen til Rønne. Turen tog cirka tre timer.

De boede på en lille campingplads ved stranden, tæt på Dueodde, som er kendt for sit hvide sand. Hver morgen stod de tidligt op og cyklede rundt på øen. Mikkel ville gerne se rundkirkerne, som er over 800 år gamle, mens Anna hellere ville besøge de små fiskerbyer med røgerier, hvor man kan købe røget sild.

En dag regnede det hele dagen. De blev i teltet og spillede kort, og om aftenen gik de på en café i Svaneke. Der mødte de et ældre par fra Tyskland, som kom til Bornholm hvert år. Parret anbefalede dem at tage til Hammershus, en gammel borgruin i den nordlige del af øen. Næste dag tog de derhen. Udsigten over havet var fantastisk, og Anna tog mange billeder.

Efter en uge skulle de hjem igen. På færgen sagde Mikkel, at det havde været den bedste ferie, han nogensinde havde haft. Anna var enig, men hun var også lidt træt af at sove i telt. "Næste gang lejer vi et sommerhus," sagde hun og smilede.

Spørgsmål (svar på dansk, hele sætninger):

1. Hvornår rejste Anna og Mikkel til Bornholm?
2. Havde de været på Bornholm før?
3. Hvordan kom de til Bornholm?
4. Hvor boede de?
5. Hvad ville Mikkel gerne se?
6. Hvad ville Anna hellere lave?
7. Hvad lavede de den dag, det regnede?
8. Hvem anbefalede dem at tage til Hammershus?
9. Hvad er Hammershus?
10. Hvorfor vil Anna leje et sommerhus næste gang?

**Key:** 1. Sidste sommer. 2. Nej, aldrig. 3. Med tog til Ystad og så med færge til Rønne. 4. På en lille campingplads ved stranden tæt på Dueodde. 5. Rundkirkerne. 6. Besøge de små fiskerbyer med røgerier (og købe røget sild). 7. De blev i teltet og spillede kort, og om aftenen gik de på café i Svaneke. 8. Et ældre par fra Tyskland. 9. En gammel borgruin i den nordlige del af Bornholm. 10. Fordi hun var træt af at sove i telt.

Geoblocking as in A1.
- **Library services**: eReolen (ereolen.dk), eReolen Go (ereolengo.dk) and Filmstriben (filmstriben.dk), free with a Danish library card (get one with MitID). Monthly loan limits are set by your municipality; reserve popular audiobooks early. See the README, Method 6.
- **Free-reading shelf**: Gyldendal's *Mini Dingo* and *Dingo* easy readers (every public library; ask for the LIX band above your A1 reader); *Rasmus Klump* (Carla and Vilhelm Hansen); the remaining *Vitello* titles; Bostrup's *Amalie og skelettyvene*; *Anders And & Co.* (the weekly Danish Donald Duck magazine; libraries keep back issues); Peter Madsen's *Valhalla* albums (from 1979; album 1 *Ulven er løs*; ask your library). Stretch: *Harry Potter og De Vises Sten* (translated by Hanna Lützen) and Roald Dahl's *Charlie og chokoladefabrikken*; check eReolen and eReolen Go for the e-book and the audiobook.
- **Films**: *Jungledyret Hugo* (1993 film) is on Netflix Denmark and rentable from the Viaplay Store or Google Play. *Krummerne* (1991) is on Nordisk Film+ and TV 2 Play and rentable from the Viaplay Store, Apple TV and SF Anytime. *Far til fire* (both the 1950s–70s classics and the 2005 onward reboot) are on Nordisk Film+ and TV 2 Play and rentable on SF Anytime. None of these are on DRTV. For *Vitello* (2018), *Ternet Ninja* (2018), *Gummi Tarzan* (1981) and *Valhalla* (1986), check Filmstriben first, then JustWatch (Denmark); streaming catalogues change often. Nordisk Film+ (about 69 kr a month) covers most of the Danish film canon and is worth a subscription from here to C2. Disney+ Denmark carries Danish audio and Danish subtitles on all Disney and Pixar animation.
- **Music**: Kim Larsen albums *Værsgo* (1973) and *Forklædt som voksen* (1986), and Gasolin', Shu-bi-dua, TV-2 and Rasmus Seebach albums for the weekly ear-lane album, on Spotify/Apple Music/YouTube. Lyrics on Genius or by searching the title plus "tekst". *Højskolesangbogen* (19th edition, 2020, Folkehøjskolernes Forening) is in print; hojskolesangbogen.dk has song information and many lyrics; *Der er et yndigt land* and *I Danmark er jeg født* are in it.
- **Danish Frequency Dictionary: Essential Vocabulary, 2500 Most Common Danish Words**, MostUsedWords (Jonas Jensen), 2019, ISBN 9789492637482; buy on Amazon. Words in frequency order, each with translation, part of speech, IPA and an example sentence.
- **AI voice conversation**: ChatGPT Voice supports Danish; Langua (languatalk.com) and TalkPal are dedicated apps with Danish. Claude's voice mode and Praktika do not list Danish at the time of writing. Any of the first three works for the habit; what matters is 10 minutes, three times a week, in Danish only.
- **Official samples**: the *Danskprøve A1/A2* information pack with sample questions and audio is free on nyidanmark.dk (search "Informationspakke danskprøven"). Prøve i Dansk 1 (A2) official example papers are free PDFs on danskogproever.dk under "Om Prøve i Dansk 1: indhold og niveau": *læseforståelse* example, *skriftlig fremstilling* example, an example oral portfolio, and examiner booklets that show how answers are marked. Prøve i Dansk 1 has no listening paper.
